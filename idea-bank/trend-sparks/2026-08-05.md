## 1. Local Workshop Builder

- **Name:** Local Workshop Builder
- **Trend signal:** WASM databases, Replit-style prompt-to-app products, Figma Make, and local-first personal software are converging around small tools that can be created quickly but need persistent state.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** Replit describes an AI coding agent that can write, test, fix, and publish apps. **Verified:** Figma AI page includes Figma Make for prompt-to-code work. **Verified:** PGlite/SQLite WASM/DuckDB-Wasm show database-grade state can live in browser contexts.
- **Gap:** Prompt-to-app tools tend to produce apps or websites; they rarely create an owned local artifact with export/restore, schema, and receipts.
- **Smallest prototype:** A static browser app that lets Max define one schema, import CSV/Markdown, save five views, and export a portable bundle.
- **Why it could spread:** People already ask AI to build tiny tools; a shareable local artifact gives them something safer than a throwaway hosted app.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Users may prefer cloud convenience and collaboration over local custody.
- **Fit for Max:** Very high; directly supports Aven/Orior/Pantom internal tools and local-first agent work.
- **Evidence label:** Supported.

## 2. Reaction Card Video Reviewer

- **Name:** Reaction Card Video Reviewer
- **Trend signal:** AI video tools are moving from novelty generation toward editable worlds, storyboards, variants, and campaign content.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** Runway describes products built on real-world intelligence to understand, simulate, and act. **Verified:** CapCut markets AI video generation with avatars, templates, text-to-video, and automated storyboarding. **Verified:** Glif positions itself as a creative agent platform for short-form videos, product shoots, and campaigns.
- **Gap:** Creator tools still lack a simple way to say what in the generated world actually changed after an edit.
- **Smallest prototype:** Upload before/after clips or stills, then hand-author expected reaction, observed reaction, residual error, and next prompt.
- **Why it could spread:** Video creators already share before/after edits and prompt recipes; Reaction Cards turn revisions into reusable proof.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Model quality may improve enough that explicit reaction receipts feel like overhead for casual creators.
- **Fit for Max:** High; extends the AI video Vibe already captured.
- **Evidence label:** Supported.

## 3. Taste Boundary Closet

- **Name:** Taste Boundary Closet
- **Trend signal:** AI styling and fashion resale are crowded with recommendations, but personal taste is often defined by rejections, almost-wears, and avoided silhouettes.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** Existing lab research has active Vibes on wardrobe simulation memory and negative taste. **Verified:** Broad AI/product movement shows assistants are being embedded into commerce and creative workflows, including Shopify Sidekick and Figma/Glif-style creation surfaces.
- **Gap:** Most fashion tools optimize suggestions, not boundary capture.
- **Smallest prototype:** A local closet log with `wore`, `almost wore`, `rejected`, `saved but never used`, and `returned` events, plus a weekly taste-boundary card.
- **Why it could spread:** People naturally share outfit decisions and "why this doesn't work" content; the product makes the negative reasoning visible.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Manual logging may be too much friction unless capture is camera-native and fast.
- **Fit for Max:** Very high; matches fashion/taste-engine interests and local-first data ownership.
- **Evidence label:** Inferred.

## 4. Meeting Memory to Personal Canon

- **Name:** Meeting Memory to Personal Canon
- **Trend signal:** AI meeting products are shifting from transcription toward durable notes, action memory, and chat that knows the user's work.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** Granola describes itself as an AI notepad for meetings with notes, actions, and memory, without a meeting bot. **Verified:** Notion AI includes AI meeting notes, enterprise search, agents, and automation.
- **Gap:** Meeting notes rarely become explicit doctrine: how the person prefers decisions, follow-up, language, boundaries, or delegated behavior.
- **Smallest prototype:** Import ten meeting notes and extract scoped Canon Cards: preference, evidence quote, expiration, counterexample, and agent instruction.
- **Why it could spread:** Teams already forward meeting summaries; canon cards are more reusable and personal than another transcript.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Extracted doctrine can become stale or invasive if review/expiration is weak.
- **Fit for Max:** High; directly connects to Aven behavioral canon and agent continuity.
- **Evidence label:** Supported.

## 5. Event Afterlife Room

- **Name:** Event Afterlife Room
- **Trend signal:** Social coordination products are becoming lightweight, visual, and event-specific rather than permanent social networks.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** Partiful markets fast event pages, RSVP tracking, text blasts, reminders, comments, photos, and ticket selling. **Verified:** Strava shows activity-specific social sharing and community motivation can work outside generic feeds.
- **Gap:** Events often die after RSVP/photos; they do not become a structured memory, group taste graph, or next-event engine.
- **Smallest prototype:** A post-event room that turns photos, comments, attendance, songs, locations, and inside jokes into a private recap page plus next-event prompts.
- **Why it could spread:** Guests already want photos and recap artifacts; hosts want the next event to be easier.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Could become another photo dump if the recap is not genuinely useful or tasteful.
- **Fit for Max:** Medium-high; good social-product lane with strong aesthetic room potential.
- **Evidence label:** Supported.

## 6. Creative Agent Tool Shelf

- **Name:** Creative Agent Tool Shelf
- **Trend signal:** Creative AI platforms are bundling many generation/editing tools behind a single prompt or agent layer.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** Glif says it orchestrates hundreds of AI tools for images, videos, music, short-form videos, product shoots, and ad campaigns. **Verified:** Figma AI includes workflow tools for imagery, video, audio, design, code, motion, slides, and whiteboarding.
- **Gap:** Users need to know which tool was used, what it is allowed to do, what it costs, and whether the output is reproducible.
- **Smallest prototype:** A local creative-tool shelf with capability receipts, sample outputs, permission scope, cost estimate, and failure notes for ten tools.
- **Why it could spread:** Creators constantly share stacks and workflows; receipts make stacks portable and trustworthy.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Tool APIs and model quality change quickly, making receipts stale.
- **Fit for Max:** High; connects to Capability Receipt Manifests and creative provenance.
- **Evidence label:** Supported.

## 7. Shopify Sidequest Agent

- **Name:** Shopify Sidequest Agent
- **Trend signal:** Commerce platforms are embedding assistants to help merchants run stores, but the daily work is still a stream of small operational quests.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** Shopify describes Sidekick as a commerce-obsessed AI assistant designed to help start, run, and grow a business. **Verified:** Notion and Replit show broader movement toward agents that automate work and build tools.
- **Gap:** Store owners need scoped, reversible missions: fix product descriptions, compare abandoned carts, draft a landing page, find broken collections, or prep a launch checklist.
- **Smallest prototype:** A local quest board that turns one store export into five safe missions with expected deltas, approval gates, and rollback notes.
- **Why it could spread:** Small merchants already live inside checklists and launch scrambles; quest cards are easier to trust than open-ended AI chat.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Shopify's native assistant may absorb enough of the job for many merchants.
- **Fit for Max:** Medium-high; Pantom/Orior-adjacent business tooling and agent receipts.
- **Evidence label:** Supported.

## 8. Instant Whiteboard With Memory

- **Name:** Instant Whiteboard With Memory
- **Trend signal:** No-signup collaborative canvases remain useful, while AI workflows want structured context instead of screenshots.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** tldraw positions itself as a free, instant collaborative whiteboard with no signup required. **Verified:** Figma AI and FigJam show AI and collaboration are merging inside design surfaces.

- **Why it could spread:** Teams already screenshot boards; structured board memory makes sharing and follow-up cleaner.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Users may not want memory in a tool whose value is low-friction ephemerality.
- **Fit for Max:** High; overlaps with tiny-world tools, dossiers, design worlds, and agent receipts.
- **Evidence label:** Supported.

## 9. Social Fitness for Creative Output

- **Name:** Social Fitness for Creative Output

- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** Strava markets tracking progress, sharing, cheering, challenges, maps, and community-powered motivation for activities. **Verified:** Glif/Runway/CapCut show short-form creative production volume is increasing.

## 10. Personal Browser Handoff Desk

- **Name:** Personal Browser Handoff Desk
- **Trend signal:** Browsers and work apps are adding AI, but users still lose task context across tabs, docs, meetings, and small automations.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** Arc markets a calmer personal browser and points users toward Dia as its next evolution. **Verified:** Notion AI offers search, generation, analysis, chat, agents, and meeting notes in one workspace. **Verified:** Granola offers meeting memory across desktop/mobile contexts.
- **Gap:** There is no calm handoff desk that collects "where I was, what I intended, what changed, what is risky next" across browser work.
- **Smallest prototype:** A local browser-side capture page with active task, relevant tabs, last meeting note, next risky action, and agent-ready handoff card.
- **Why it could spread:** Knowledge workers already send themselves links and notes; a handoff card is a sharper ritual for resuming and delegating.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Browser vendors may own this layer, or privacy permissions may make capture too invasive.
- **Fit for Max:** Very high; agent peripheral vision, flight recorders, and personal AI canon all point here.
- **Evidence label:** Supported.
