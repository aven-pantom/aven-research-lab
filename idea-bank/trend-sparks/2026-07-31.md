# Trend Sparks — 2026-07-31

## Date

2026-07-31

## Source scan

Scanned GitHub API metadata and arXiv API results for visible movement around AI wardrobes, virtual try-on, taste/recommendation systems, local-first personal AI, creator provenance, agent interfaces, workflow capture, and drone/operations utilities. The strongest current movement remained around agent tools and local personal software, while the most interesting consumer signal appeared in taste systems: fashion tools keep chasing try-on/recommendation, but adjacent music/movie tools expose skips, swipes, and preference traces as the behavior loop.

GitHub star counts and repo descriptions are weak public signals, not proof of revenue, retention, or product quality. arXiv abstracts are evidence of active research direction, not consumer demand.

## Selection logic

The 10 Sparks favor products with a visible behavior loop, a smallest prototype Max could build without platform dependence, and a non-generic primitive: negative taste capture, agent receipts, creator workflow cards, local-first personal memory, lightweight drone ops legibility, and weird consumer utilities. Excluded: generic AI stylists, cloned feeds, virtual try-on novelty apps, monetization-only funnels, and protocol-first ideas without a capture loop.

## 10 Trend Sparks

### 1. Almost-Wear Diary

- **Trend signal:** AI wardrobe and virtual try-on repos are appearing, while arXiv fashion recommendation work continues around personalization and aesthetic compatibility.
- **Existing proof:** GitHub scan found `WardrobeMax`, `aina`, `tryon_ITI---Master`, and other AI wardrobe/try-on repos; arXiv returned CFALR (`2606.13001v1`) and AesRec (`2602.03416v1`) on personalized/aesthetic outfit recommendation.
- **Gap:** Wardrobe apps suggest outfits but rarely capture the high-value moment where a user almost wears something, then rejects it.
- **Smallest prototype:** Mobile/local web diary with photo, candidate outfit, final rejected item, one-tap reason, context, and weekly “style boundary” summary.
- **Why it could spread:** “What I almost wore” is socially legible, intimate, and more interesting than another outfit-of-the-day post.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Manual capture may be too much friction unless it rides an existing mirror/photo habit.
- **Fit for Max:** Very strong: fashion taste engines, local-first personal software, social primitives.
- **Evidence label:** Supported for research/tool movement; Inferred for consumer loop

### 2. Taste Boundary Onboarding

- **Trend signal:** Recommendation products increasingly use conversational agents, swipes, and taste identities across music, movies, anime, and shopping.
- **Existing proof:** GitHub scan found `WatchIt`, `movie_analysis_agent`, `mirubox`, `kocteau`, and `tunelog`; `tunelog` explicitly learns from skips/replays rather than ratings.
- **Gap:** New social/style apps still onboard with likes and follows, not refusals and “not for me” boundaries.
- **Smallest prototype:** Ask for five loves and five hard refusals; generate a portable Taste Boundary Card with positive and negative anchors.
- **Why it could spread:** People like declaring taste boundaries because they feel more personal than generic interests.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Users may perform taste for identity rather than provide useful data.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong: social products, portable taste graphs, brand worlds.
- **Evidence label:** Inferred from verified adjacent tool behavior

### 3. Reference Board That Notices Unused Saves

- **Trend signal:** Creator workflows accumulate saved references, screenshots, and boards, but most tools do not distinguish useful inspiration from aspirational clutter.
- **Existing proof:** Prior lab provenance/taste work plus current provenance repos (`oprindo-sdk-js`, `oprindo-sdk-py`, `provenance-ci`) show developer interest in content-local process metadata.
- **Gap:** Creative tools track what was saved, not what was actually used, ignored, mutated, or rejected.
- **Smallest prototype:** Local reference board with four events: saved, used, ignored, rejected; export a process card for one final artifact.
- **Why it could spread:** Designers/creators can show a beautiful “sources that survived” receipt.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Existing boards may be “good enough”; creators may not want process accountability.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong: creative tools, provenance sandboxes, Orior/Pantom process.
- **Evidence label:** Supported for provenance movement; Inferred for use/reject loop

### 4. Agent Action Risk Strip

- **Trend signal:** Agent tools continue to move into terminals, browsers, MCP connectors, and IDE workflows.
- **Existing proof:** Previous scan showed high public attention around Claude Code, browser-use, Cline, AutoGen, OmniParser, HumanLayer, and Graphiti; current local-first agent repos continue to appear.
- **Gap:** Users need a peripheral “what surface is the agent touching, and is the next action reversible?” strip, not another full dashboard.
- **Smallest prototype:** Menubar/web strip showing current agent phase, touched files/sites, next irreversible boundary, pause, and receipt link.
- **Why it could spread:** It turns background autonomy anxiety into a glanceable object.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Vendor agent UIs may absorb the obvious controls.
- **Fit for Max:** Very strong: agent interface primitives, Pantom/Orior operations.
- **Evidence label:** Supported

### 5. Screen Recording to Regression Test

- **Trend signal:** Repos are emerging that turn screen recordings into workflows, skills, tests, or agent traces.
- **Existing proof:** GitHub scan found `AutoTestAI` describing screen-recording-to-Playwright test generation; the prior run found `skillsmith`, `recit`, and `record` for screen-recording-to-agent skill/local trace workflows.
- **Gap:** Screen recordings become tutorials or evidence, but not reusable tests that protect a creator/operator's workflow.
- **Smallest prototype:** Upload a 30-second task recording, mark intended final state, export a Playwright smoke test plus Markdown process card.
- **Why it could spread:** “Turn your demo into a test” has immediate before/after value for builders.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** UI OCR/action inference is brittle; manual annotation may be necessary.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong: agent tools, creator workflows, transition tests.
- **Evidence label:** Verified for repo signals; Inferred for spread loop

### 6. Local Personal AI Inventory

- **Trend signal:** Local-first personal AI repos continue to appear around Markdown, owned context, project memory, and personal software environments.
- **Existing proof:** GitHub scan found `wolffish-mobile`, `wolffish-app`, `Personal_Intelligence_System`, and `lamarck` describing local-first or personal AI/software environments.
- **Gap:** Users cannot see which files, memories, receipts, taste cards, and tools their personal AI actually owns.
- **Smallest prototype:** Local inventory page that lists personal AI assets by source, age, permissions, last used, and delete/export controls.
- **Why it could spread:** People need a home screen for “what my AI knows about me” before trusting deeper memory.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Too infrastructural unless attached to a concrete use case like style, projects, or content.
- **Fit for Max:** Very strong: local-first/personal software, agent-maintained tools.
- **Evidence label:** Supported

### 7. Return Reason Camera

- **Trend signal:** Fashion e-commerce and AI wardrobe repos mention recommendations, virtual try-on, and return verification, but returns are usually operational data for retailers.
- **Existing proof:** `SRU_SRUCODERS` description included AI-assisted return verification; arXiv fashion papers emphasize recommendation/compatibility and retail try-on contexts.
- **Gap:** A return is a high-quality personal taste/fitting signal, but users lose it after the refund.
- **Smallest prototype:** Before returning an item, capture one photo, reason tags, fit notes, and “would buy if” conditions into a local taste card.
- **Why it could spread:** Turns frustrating returns into better future shopping and shareable anti-haul content.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Users may only want the refund done quickly.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong: fashion, consumer utility, taste memory.
- **Evidence label:** Inferred from verified retail/try-on signals

### 8. Creator Provenance Checkpoint Button

- **Trend signal:** Small C2PA/content-credentials repos keep appearing around SDKs, CI, and publishing pipeline verification.
- **Existing proof:** GitHub scan found `hexrift/oprindo-sdk-js`, `hexrift/oprindo-sdk-py`, `borborich/provenance-ci`, `provemark/content-credentials`, and `llama-index-tools-chronoverify`.
- **Gap:** Provenance tools focus on final content credentials; creators need beautiful checkpoints during work.
- **Smallest prototype:** Button that saves a checkpoint card with sources, current draft, edits since last checkpoint, public/private notes, and exportable receipt.
- **Why it could spread:** Creators can show process without exposing raw files.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Provenance may feel like compliance homework unless the card is useful for memory and storytelling.
- **Fit for Max:** Very strong: creative tools, Orior/Pantom evidence, provenance sandboxes.
- **Evidence label:** Supported

### 9. Drone Ops Neighbor Window

- **Trend signal:** Small drone operations/logbook/weather repos continue to appear, while civil drone activity still lacks calm local legibility.
- **Existing proof:** GitHub scan found `DroneWheather`, `drone-ops-app`, `DroneHub-App`, and other drone app repos; prior lab work established Drone Intent Cards and FAA Remote ID as context.
- **Gap:** Remote ID handles identification; neighbors/event staff need a temporary human-readable purpose and contact window.
- **Smallest prototype:** Expiring local web card for one drone operation: time window, purpose, area, privacy note, contact, and emergency stop/contact route.
- **Why it could spread:** Useful for creators, real estate, events, and small operators who want fewer awkward confrontations.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Could be confused with regulatory compliance or expose sensitive operational details.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong: drones/aviation interfaces, civil autonomy.
- **Evidence label:** Inferred from verified repo movement and prior Remote ID context

### 10. Swipe-to-Prompt Taste Sampler

- **Trend signal:** Swipe interfaces and preference-aware agents remain common in new movie/anime/recommendation projects.
- **Existing proof:** `WatchIt` describes a Tinder-style swipe interface for dynamic taste profiles; `movie_analysis_agent` and `mirubox` emphasize preference-aware recommendation.
- **Gap:** Swipe data usually stays inside the app; creators/builders need a small exportable prompt/taste profile from a quick sampling session.
- **Smallest prototype:** 60-second swipe session across images/songs/products that exports a private Taste Prompt and negative boundary list.
- **Why it could spread:** It is an easy social mechanic: “take my taste sampler” or “make one for your brand world.”
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Swipe mechanics can become shallow and noisy; the export must be visibly useful.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong: viral/social app products, brand worlds, taste engines.
- **Evidence label:** Inferred from verified repo descriptions

## Anti-slop filter

Rejected:

- “AI wardrobe stylist” — too generic unless it captures almost-wears, returns, and refusal boundaries.
- “Virtual try-on social network” — crowded novelty surface with weak retention primitive.
- “Universal taste graph protocol” — protocol-first; capture the event grammar first.
- “Agent dashboard” — generic unless narrowed to reversible action/risk strip.
- “Content credentials app” — compliance wrapper unless checkpoints become creator memory.
- “Drone social feed” — dual-use and privacy risk; use expiring neighbor cards instead.
