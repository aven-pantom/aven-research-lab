# Rejected Path — AI Bots as Fake Users

## Why it initially seemed promising

The tempting headline is obvious: AI can solve social-app cold start by filling the feed with bots.

That framing is commercially attractive because cold start is one of the hardest consumer-social problems, and synthetic content can create immediate activity.

## What evidence weakened it

**Verified:** The research sources support simulation, populated prototypes, believable agents, and controlled synthetic social networks. They do not validate covert fake users as a healthy product strategy.

**Supported:** The history of bots in social media is strongly associated with spam, manipulation, fraud, astroturfing, and trust collapse.

**Inferred:** If users discover that apparent peers were synthetic and undisclosed, the product’s social proof becomes evidence of deception.

## Which assumption failed

The failed assumption was:

```text
activity itself solves cold start
```

The better assumption is:

```text
legible participation scaffolding can reduce cold start if users understand what is synthetic and why it exists
```

## Whether it may become viable later

Covert fake users should stay rejected.

A disclosed synthetic inhabitant layer may become viable if paired with:

- clear visual grammar,
- explicit roles,
- synthetic activity controls,
- memory boundaries,
- influence caps,
- user inspection/muting,
- retreat conditions once human density is sufficient.

## Useful remnants

- synthetic agents can provide ambient prompts,
- simulated users can red-team moderation,
- role-bound inhabitants can host onboarding,
- fake engagement metrics should be forbidden,
- synthetic participation needs a product-level contract.

## Future signal that should trigger reconsideration

Reconsider only if a live product demonstrates durable retention and user trust with fully disclosed synthetic inhabitants, not merely engagement lift from hidden bots.
