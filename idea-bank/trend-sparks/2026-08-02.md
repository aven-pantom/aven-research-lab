## Selection logic

Priority went to Max-aligned lanes: agent tools, local-first personal software, creative tools, fashion/taste engines, aviation interfaces, social products, creator funnels, and weird consumer utilities. I rejected generic AI wrappers, direct clones of dominant apps, and monetization-only ideas without a behavior loop.

## Anti-slop filter

Rejected patterns:

- “AI assistant for X” without a specific repeated behavior.
- CapCut/ChatGPT/Notion clones.
- Generic trend dashboards without source receipts.
- Drone tools that imply operational safety or routing authority.
- Fashion apps that only generate outfits without remembering real wear/reject behavior.
- Agent tools that hide permissions, receipts, or rollback.

## 1. Reflex Path Recorder

- **Trend signal:** Computer-use agent research is naming timing failures: agents can choose the correct action after the GUI window has closed.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** arXiv API surfaced `Why Are GUI Agents Correct but Late?` (`2607.28399v1`) plus local CUA inference and GUI transition papers on 2026-08-02. GitHub metadata showed high active attention around `browser-use`, `OmniParser`, `Agent-S`, `OpenInterpreter`, and `open-swe`.
- **Gap:** Agent logs show what was attempted, but not which short-lived interface reflexes were armed, why they fired, and whether they expired safely.
- **Smallest prototype:** Local Playwright microbench plus `reflex-receipt.json` viewer for modal escape, wait-for-stability, stop-on-wrong-surface, and undo reflexes.
- **Why it could spread:** Agent builders need trust primitives that are smaller than full security platforms and more concrete than chat summaries.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Reflex automation can become unsafe if it bypasses scope or approval gates.
- **Fit for Max:** Very high; direct Hermes/Aven, agent UX, local-first, flight-recorder, and undo-envelope adjacency.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**

## 2. Phone Cockpit for Local Agents

- **Trend signal:** Developers are trying to control persistent computer/coding agents away from the main desktop.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** HN/Algolia scan surfaced `WhipDesk – Control your full dev machine from your phone`, `CobaltCode – Dedicated persistent computer for Codex`, and `Crew, a local collaborative (people and agents) IDE`.
- **Gap:** Current remote-control tools expose the machine, not a calm agent cockpit with phase, risk, approvals, receipts, rollback, and stop controls.
- **Smallest prototype:** Mobile web panel for one local agent: current task, touched files/surfaces, next risky action, approve/hold/stop, and trace link.
- **Why it could spread:** It gives builders a way to let agents work while staying lightly in command.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Developers may prefer full SSH/VNC or native IDE control until agents are reliable enough.
- **Fit for Max:** Very high; strong agent-control and Hermes operating-loop fit.
- **Evidence label:** **Interesting pattern**

## 3. Intent-to-Motion Receipt

- **Trend signal:** Prompt-to-video and prompt-to-motion editing are becoming creator workflow material, not just model demos.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** HN/Algolia surfaced `Moto – a new AI video editor with editable prompt-to-motion graphics`; iTunes Search surfaced CapCut, Videoleap, and Vids AI with large rating counts.
- **Gap:** Video tools export clips, but not portable records of prompt, references, timing, motion rules, rejected branches, approvals, and variant intent.
- **Smallest prototype:** Sidecar `motion-receipt.md/json` for a 10–15 second short with timeline beats and reproducibility confidence.
- **Why it could spread:** Creators can share “how this was made” as reusable structure rather than only a final export.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Many creators may value speed/output more than process memory.
- **Fit for Max:** Very high; direct Pantom/Orior and creative provenance lane.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**

## 4. Almost-Wear Resale Queue

- **Trend signal:** Closet, outfit planning, and resale behavior remain visible mobile categories.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** iTunes Search surfaced Whering, Fits, Combyne, SimpleCloset, Closetly, Depop-adjacent and wardrobe apps with rating counts; prior 2026-08-02 Apple feed scan showed resale/closet apps in charts.
- **Gap:** Wardrobe apps log items, but rarely turn almost-wears, returns, duplicates, and fit doubts into sell/repair/tailor/keep decisions.
- **Smallest prototype:** Local closet queue where each almost-wear creates one card: why rejected, what it competes with, sell/alter/hold action, marketplace search link.
- **Why it could spread:** Closet guilt and resale intent are recurring; the product turns indecision into a shareable/actionable queue.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Manual capture must be nearly frictionless or it dies after onboarding.
- **Fit for Max:** Very high; fashion taste engine and negative-taste memory fit.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**

## 5. Social Habit Proof Ring

- **Trend signal:** Social habit trackers and steps-with-friends apps continue to package lightweight accountability as social behavior.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** iTunes Search surfaced `WIP: Social Habit Tracker`, `Noom Vibe: Steps with Friends`, and `HabitShare` under social/productivity/fitness behavior.
- **Gap:** Habit apps track streaks, but not proof artifacts and micro-context: what changed, what got easier, what almost broke the streak, and who witnessed it.
- **Smallest prototype:** 7-day private ring: daily proof card, one friction note, one witness reaction, weekly recap artifact.
- **Why it could spread:** Small groups create pressure and recap content without needing a large public network.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Habit social loops often decay after novelty; needs a real identity/status hook.
- **Fit for Max:** Medium-high; social product loop and creator/content funnel adjacency.
- **Evidence label:** **Interesting pattern**

## 6. Moodboard With Taste Boundaries

- **Trend signal:** Moodboard and visual planning tools remain common, while taste engines are moving from likes toward richer preference data.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** iTunes Search surfaced Moodboard, Morpholio Board, and Milanote; existing lab Vibes show a repeated pattern around negative taste, portable taste graphs, and camera-native palettes.
- **Gap:** Boards preserve inspiration but not boundaries: too polished, too sterile, too loud, too safe, too brand-like, never use again.
- **Smallest prototype:** Drop 20 references; mark 5 as “wrong but close”; export a Taste Boundary Card with positive and negative aesthetic rules.
- **Why it could spread:** Designers and creators often know what they do not want; boundary cards are shareable with collaborators and agents.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Could become a prettier note app unless the boundary extraction is genuinely useful.
- **Fit for Max:** Very high; taste systems, design tools, Orior/Pantom research fit.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**

## 7. Drone Scanner Social Norm Card

- **Trend signal:** Drone/airspace visibility apps exist, but public understanding of what a nearby drone is doing remains weak.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** iTunes Search surfaced `Drone Scanner` and `Drone Scanner (gen.2)`; prior lab research confirmed FAA UTM context and Remote ID/airspace-manners adjacency.
- **Gap:** Detection alone says “a drone is near,” not whether it is permitted, what kind of intent is appropriate to disclose, or what a bystander should do.
- **Smallest prototype:** Non-operational educational card: drone seen nearby, what Remote ID can/cannot imply, local norms, report/ignore guidance, privacy note.
- **Why it could spread:** Public anxiety around drones needs legible norms before dense autonomy arrives.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Regulatory and safety-sensitive; must avoid tactical tracking or operational claims.
- **Fit for Max:** High; aviation interface, public autonomy legibility, and airspace manners fit.
- **Evidence label:** **Interesting pattern**

## 8. Flight Tracker for Personal Trips

- **Trend signal:** Flight tracking remains a mainstream high-utility behavior.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** iTunes Search surfaced Flightradar24 and FlightAware with large rating counts.
- **Gap:** Flight trackers show aircraft, but normal travelers need a trip-specific situational card: inbound aircraft risk, airport weather, gate churn, connection walk, bag timing, pickup coordination.
- **Smallest prototype:** Forward one itinerary email; generate a calm trip card with live links and “what matters next” updates.
- **Why it could spread:** Travel anxiety creates repeat checking; a personal trip card is easier to share than a flight-tracking map.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Incumbents and airline apps own much of the data; email parsing can be brittle.
- **Fit for Max:** Medium-high; aviation UX and personal software fit.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**

## 9. Screen Recording to Skill Card

- **Trend signal:** Screen recording and work-recording tools are visible across consumer apps and agent-maker communities.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** iTunes Search surfaced high-rating-count screen recorder apps; HN/Algolia surfaced Screenpipe-style work recording and local agent workspace tools.
- **Gap:** Recordings are heavy artifacts; agents need distilled skill cards: trigger, steps, exceptions, approvals, source surfaces, and safe replay bounds.
- **Smallest prototype:** Upload/record a 60-second workflow; produce Markdown Skill Card plus source timestamps and confidence labels.
- **Why it could spread:** Creators, operators, and founders already record Looms; turning them into agent-readable procedures is a natural next step.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Skill extraction can hallucinate hidden steps unless grounded in timestamps and user review.
- **Fit for Max:** Very high; Aven/Hermes, local-first agent training, and content funnels fit.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**

## 10. Paid Utility Ritual Radar

- **Trend signal:** Narrow utility apps with strong use rituals still attract paid or high-intent users.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** iTunes Search and prior Apple feed scans surfaced flight trackers, screen recorders, wardrobe tools, weather/aviation utilities, camera/video tools, and specialist creative apps with visible rating counts.
- **Gap:** Product research overweights viral free apps and misses paid rituals where users already accept focused, non-generic tools.
- **Smallest prototype:** Weekly Markdown radar of 25 paid/high-intent utilities: user ritual, data dependency, missing agent/local companion, proof, and prototype wedge.
- **Why it could spread:** Builders want specific product seams, not trend sludge.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** More research instrument than app unless packaged as a public scout/newsletter/tool.
- **Fit for Max:** High; Orior-style product discovery and weird consumer utility taste.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**
