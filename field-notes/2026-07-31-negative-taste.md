# Field Notes — 2026-07-31 Negative Taste Signals

## Signal

Fashion AI appears active in two layers: research papers improving virtual try-on/outfit recommendation and tiny new repos making AI wardrobes or try-on shopping assistants. The product surface is crowded and mostly obvious. The useful signal is underneath: none of these tools will feel personal unless they learn from the moments where the user says no.

## Raw observations

- Several 2026 GitHub repos describe AI wardrobes, virtual try-on, and shopping recommendation, but observed star counts were mostly 0–1.
- `tunelog` is a sharper adjacent signal because it names skips/replays as the taste substrate and removes ratings.
- arXiv fashion recommendation papers focus on preference, aesthetics, compatibility, and data sparsity; this supports the idea that better taste data may matter as much as model architecture.
- “Almost wore” feels like the wardrobe equivalent of a music skip. The item was close enough to be considered, then failed at the last moment.
- “Saved but never used” may be the creative-reference equivalent of cart abandonment.

## Questions

- Could a taste app start by asking for five refusals rather than five likes?
- Is a return reason more valuable than a purchase receipt?
- Can personal negative signals stay local while still onboarding into social/fashion products?
- Which negative signal is easiest to capture without killing the behavior loop?

## Rejected paths during scan

- Generic virtual try-on app: too crowded and positive-signal dominated.
- “AI stylist chat” wrapper: no new primitive.
- Universal taste graph protocol: premature before finding the right event grammar.
- Platform-scale recommender clone: outside the lab’s leverage; the opportunity is personal/local, not another feed.
