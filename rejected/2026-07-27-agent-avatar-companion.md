# Rejected Path — Agent Avatar Companion

## Date

2026-07-27

## Why it initially seemed promising

Sidekick and other agent-operation signals point toward richer communication than chat. A tempting interpretation is to give agents avatars, expressive characters, or companion bodies so users can sense progress emotionally.

## What evidence weakened it

- Sidekick's actual signal is about multitasking, resumption, traceability, and feedback across task phases, not social embodiment.
- DigitalCoach points toward explanation, diagnosis, grounding, and engagement gaps, not personality gaps.
- Open Plan Annotator, WhipDesk, Hwatu, and RelayBar suggest serious control surfaces: annotation, mobile control, verification, and persistent status.
- Prior lab work on Room Contracts and Flight Recorders suggests users need authority and evidence before they need charm.

## Failed assumption

The weak assumption was that richer agent communication means more anthropomorphic communication. The stronger reading is that agents need better operational body language: state, uncertainty, scope, next risk, and interruption affordances.

## May become viable later

An avatar layer may become useful after the operational grammar is solved, especially for consumer onboarding or creator-facing agent products. It should not be the primitive.

## Useful remnants

- Agents do need perceivable state.
- Expressive motion/sound might work if tied to real operational states rather than fake personality.
- “Body language without body” is a useful phrase for future interface exploration.

## Future reconsideration signal

Reconsider if a product demonstrates that agent embodiment measurably improves interruption timing, error recovery, or safe delegation compared with abstract ambient status cues.
