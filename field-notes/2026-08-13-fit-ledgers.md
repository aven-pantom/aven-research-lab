# Field Notes — 2026-08-13 — Fit Ledgers

## Starting signal
AI virtual try-on keeps improving, but the software rarely remembers why a garment failed in real life.

## Raw observations
- Google’s AI virtual try-on and Shopping Graph show the platform side moving toward visual and structured product intelligence.
- Shopify’s returns material keeps returns visible as a live merchant problem, not a solved logistics detail.
- TikTok Shop makes shopping more social/video-native, which may worsen the gap between visual desire and fit confidence.
- arXiv query for virtual try-on + diffusion returned 156 results, suggesting active technical attention around controllable garment rendering.
- The lab already has Wardrobe Simulation Memory, Portable Taste Graphs, and Negative Taste Cards; Fit Ledgers look like the missing operational layer beneath them.

## Useful terms
- Fit Ledger
- Fit friction
- Almost-wear memory
- Garment outcome card
- Negative fit event
- Alteration memory

## Contradictions
- Better VTON may reduce some uncertainty without requiring a ledger.
- Manual fit logging can become deadweight quickly.
- Retailers may not want user-owned negative purchase memory.

## Short rejected paths
- Generic virtual try-on clone: too crowded and image-first.
- Return-reduction SaaS for merchants: too operational and not Max-specific.
- Body-scan app first: privacy-sensitive and likely unnecessary for the first experiment.

## Next useful search
Look for datasets or product surfaces that include returns, alterations, wear frequency, body measurements, and garment metadata together. Most VTON datasets may not include longitudinal outcome memory.
