## Anti-slop filter

Each spark names proof, gap, smallest prototype, spread mechanism, risk, Max fit, and evidence label. Evidence labels reflect current scans and inference rather than fabricated users, revenue, downloads, or metrics.

## 1. Radio Weather Card

- **Trend signal:** Current arXiv cluster around low-altitude route-level radio maps, foundational aerial radio maps, 3D radio-map estimation, and communication-aware UAV control.
- **Existing proof:** arXiv:2602.10736, 2604.17362, 2605.29538, 2607.00288, plus older cellular-connected UAV radio-map path-planning papers.
- **Gap:** Drone interfaces treat connectivity as signal bars, hidden constraints, or route optimization rather than readable evidence with freshness, uncertainty, and residuals.
- **Smallest prototype:** 20 synthetic, simulator-only Radio Weather Cards with expected margin, source density, freshness, observed residual, and conservative next constraint.
- **Why it could spread:** Drone builders and autonomy-interface people can share “why this route class was risky” receipts without exposing real routes.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Could look like operational authorization; source data may be too sparse or proprietary for useful deployment.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong Notrex/N1 Scout, aviation-interface, Weather Autopsy, and trace-card alignment.
- **Evidence label:** Verified / Inferred.

## 2. Phone-Agent Privacy Drill

- **Trend signal:** Current arXiv scan surfaced mobile-agent privacy and evaluation papers, including `Do Phone-Use Agents Respect Your Privacy?`, `(A)I Sees What You Don't`, and mobile-agent judge benchmarking.
- **Existing proof:** arXiv results for mobile agents + permission/privacy; existing lab Vibes on Mobile Permission Receipts and Attention Airlocks.
- **Gap:** Phone-agent demos show task completion, but users need fast drills that reveal app-boundary crossings, hidden observations, and overbroad touches.
- **Smallest prototype:** A local mock-phone web app with 10 tasks and a Privacy Drill Card after each run: surfaces touched, private fields seen, forbidden action risk, and receipt clarity.
- **Why it could spread:** Everyone understands “did it look at something it should not?” faster than benchmark scores.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Platform vendors may absorb receipts; mock drills may not transfer to real mobile OS automation.
- **Fit for Max:** Direct Aven/Hermes/mobile-agent trust lane.
- **Evidence label:** Supported / Inferred.

## 3. Local Recipe Workshop

- **Trend signal:** GitHub current scan for local-first apps surfaced active personal/local app repositories; browser database/PWA movement remains visible.
- **Existing proof:** GitHub search returned recently updated local-first app and PWA/SQLite repositories; previous source base around OPFS, SQLite WASM, and Single-File Workshop Cards.
- **Gap:** Recipe apps are cloud/social/search-heavy, but serious cooks need private versioned ledgers: substitutions, timing, equipment, failures, cost, and guests.
- **Smallest prototype:** Single-file browser recipe workshop with 30 recipes, change receipts, pantry notes, export/restore, and “what changed since last cook” view.
- **Why it could spread:** Recipes already spread socially; a beautiful local “version card” can be shared without forcing an account.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Manual logging could feel like work; incumbent recipe managers may already satisfy casual users.
- **Fit for Max:** Weird consumer utility + local-first workshop primitive.
- **Evidence label:** Supported / Inferred.

## 4. Fit-Friction Resale Queue

- **Trend signal:** GitHub scan surfaced active virtual try-on/fashion and wardrobe app repositories; prior lab research found Fit Ledgers and negative taste events stronger than generic try-on.
- **Existing proof:** Repositories including `Bootique-Design-Studio`, `shop-tryon-skill`, `VirtualFit_AI`, and wardrobe apps were recently updated in search results.
- **Gap:** Wardrobe tools help see outfits, but rarely turn repeated fit friction into resale, alteration, or keep decisions.
- **Smallest prototype:** Mobile list that converts “almost-wore / tugged / returned / altered” events into a resale queue with reasons and listing draft fields.
- **Why it could spread:** People already complain privately about clothes that almost work; resale gives the diary a payoff.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Resale workflows are fragmented; users may not log enough negative fit data.
- **Fit for Max:** Fashion/taste-engine lane with a concrete behavior loop.
- **Evidence label:** Supported / Inferred.

## 5. Screen Bug Repro Clip

- **Trend signal:** GitHub scan for AI screen recording surfaced recently updated repos including `repro-clip`; agents and QA increasingly depend on replayable UI evidence.
- **Existing proof:** Current GitHub results plus existing lab Vibes on Flight Recorders, Transition Tests, Shared Workbenches, and Shadow Runs.
- **Gap:** Bug reports still lose the state transition: what was expected, what changed, what was stale, and what evidence proves it.
- **Smallest prototype:** Browser extension or local web recorder that captures a 20-second repro clip plus Transition Card fields and a redacted share bundle.
- **Why it could spread:** Developers, designers, and clients already send screen recordings; structured repro cards make them actionable.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Privacy/redaction is hard; existing QA tools may own enterprise distribution.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong product-design/dev-tool/agent-observability fit.
- **Evidence label:** Supported / Inferred.

## 6. MCP Tool Expiry Shelf

- **Trend signal:** GitHub current scan for MCP agent tools returned thousands of repositories and recently updated tool projects.
- **Existing proof:** GitHub MCP/tool search; existing Vibes on Capability Receipt Manifests, Credential Escrow, and Eagerness Governors.
- **Gap:** Tool installs accumulate authority, but users and agents lack a calm shelf showing last used, authority scope, expiry, revocation, and stale risk.
- **Smallest prototype:** Local manifest viewer for 10 mock MCP tools with expiry timers, allowed actions, forbidden actions, sample receipt, and revoke note.
- **Why it could spread:** Agent power users are already collecting tools; expiry gives them a visible maintenance ritual.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Registry/platform layers may standardize this; developers may ignore extra metadata.
- **Fit for Max:** Direct Hermes/agent tooling and product primitive fit.
- **Evidence label:** Supported / Inferred.

## 7. Reaction Cut Desk

- **Trend signal:** GitHub current scan for AI video editor surfaced many recently updated projects; AI video workflows are moving from generation to editing/review.
- **Existing proof:** GitHub results such as `AI_basketball_games_video_editor`, `monke`, and `YapCut`; existing lab Vibes on Intent Timelines and Reaction Cards.
- **Gap:** AI video tools produce clips but do not preserve what changed when a beat, object, camera move, or reference was altered.
- **Smallest prototype:** Upload two short clips or mock frames, write one intended edit, then generate a Reaction Cut Card: stimulus, protected invariant, expected consequence, observed difference, next prompt.
- **Why it could spread:** Creators share before/after clips constantly; a reaction card makes critique remixable.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Requires visual comparison quality; creators may prefer speed over structured review.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong creative-tool and provenance lane.
- **Evidence label:** Supported / Inferred.

## 8. Wardrobe Packing Constraint Solver

- **Trend signal:** GitHub current scan surfaced wardrobe apps and fashion tools; travel/packing remains a recurring consumer workflow with taste, weather, and constraint complexity.
- **Existing proof:** Recently updated wardrobe repositories plus existing Fit Ledger / Wardrobe Simulation Memory Vibes.
- **Gap:** Packing apps list items, but rarely use fit friction, outfit confidence, negative taste, laundry, events, and weather as constraints.
- **Smallest prototype:** Local packing board for one trip: 20 garments, event cards, weather bands, laundry constraints, fit-friction warnings, and shareable packing receipt.
- **Why it could spread:** Travel outfits are social, visual, and anxiety-heavy; receipts/screenshots are shareable.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Too seasonal; users may fall back to notes/checklists.
- **Fit for Max:** Fashion/taste engine with a crisp consumer utility loop.
- **Evidence label:** Supported / Inferred.

## 9. Browser Agent Sandbox Ticket

- **Trend signal:** GitHub current scan for browser agents surfaced broad active repo movement; mobile-agent/privacy papers show agent action surfaces are creating new risk.
- **Existing proof:** GitHub browser-agent results and lab Vibes on Borrowed Rooms, Shadow Runs, Action Maps, and Credential Lease Cards.
- **Gap:** Browser-agent sessions lack a single ticket declaring which site, data, verbs, credentials, and expiry are in bounds.
- **Smallest prototype:** A one-page Sandbox Ticket generator that exports a JSON/Markdown contract and receipt template before any browser-agent run.
- **Why it could spread:** People delegating web tasks need a copy-pasteable boundary object now, before standards settle.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Without runtime enforcement it may be paperwork; browsers/agent clients may build native controls.
- **Fit for Max:** Direct agent-interface and trust-UX fit.
- **Evidence label:** Supported / Inferred.

## 10. Campus Airspace Explainer

- **Trend signal:** Drone GCS repositories remain active, while low-altitude airspace research increasingly combines weather, connectivity, intent, and autonomy evidence.
- **Existing proof:** GitHub scan surfaced `qgroundcontrol` and mission-control repositories; arXiv scan surfaced radio/weather-adjacent low-altitude papers; existing Vibes include Airspace Manners and Weather Autopsies.
- **Gap:** Non-experts near drone operations need a simple civil explanation surface: why a drone is here, what it is not doing, what condition changed, and when it leaves.
- **Smallest prototype:** Static public explainer cards for fictional campus/warehouse drone operations: purpose, time window, privacy boundary, weather/radio note, contact, and expiration.
- **Why it could spread:** Local trust depends on legibility; cards can be printed, posted, linked, or shown on-site.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Real operations require legal/compliance review; public cards could create liability or reveal too much.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong Notrex/civil drone product-language lane.
- **Evidence label:** Supported / Inferred.
