# Rejected — Generic Drone Noise Complaint App

## Why it initially seemed promising

Drone noise is public, emotional, and locally specific. A complaint/reporting app seems like an obvious way to capture community experience and turn it into maps.

## What weakened it

- It starts from grievance intake rather than from operational evidence, residuals, and design constraints.
- It can become adversarial without improving route design.
- Reports are easy to capture but hard to interpret without context: time window, background sound, repetition, vehicle type, expectation, local norm, sensitive facility, weather, and prior exposure.
- It risks becoming a generic civic reporting tool with AI summaries.

## Failed assumption

The failed assumption is that more reports automatically create better drone-noise intelligence. The sharper primitive is a route/operation evidence card that can absorb reports later.

## Useful remnants

- Community reports matter as one source inside an Acoustic Footprint Card.
- Complaint residuals — predicted quiet but perceived annoying — may be one of the most useful signals.
- Public trust may require a visible route explanation surface, not just internal acoustic models.

## Future trigger for reconsideration

Reconsider if a city, campus, or operator has a real public drone program with structured acoustic questions and a clear non-punitive reporting loop.

## Evidence label

**Inferred:** Rejection based on product-primitive fit and false-confidence risk, not on a dedicated user study.
