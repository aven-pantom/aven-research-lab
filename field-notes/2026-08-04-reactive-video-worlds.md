# Field Notes — 2026-08-04 — Reactive Video Worlds

## Starting signal

A fresh arXiv result, `WorldExam: Benchmarking World Models from Apparent Appearance to Inherent Reactivity` (`2608.02603v1`, 2026-08-03), frames controllable video generation as world modeling and says evaluation must go beyond visual quality and explicit instruction fulfillment.

## Notes

- **Verified:** The WorldExam abstract names four levels: Visual Quality, Control Adherence, Spatial Consistency, and World Reactivity.
- **Verified:** The abstract reports 1,474 cases across eight tasks and evaluation of 20 representative models.
- **Verified:** The abstract says camera-driven models excel at camera control but do not support dynamic interaction; action-driven models control subjects more precisely but often leave the world unresponsive; language-driven models perform better on interaction but follow complex controls less faithfully.
- **Verified:** GitHub Search API surfaced current agent-drivable video editors using MCP, REST, JSON timelines, command logs, Remotion, FFmpeg, and local-first editing patterns.
- **Inferred:** Creative tooling may soon split into two layers: timeline control and world-reaction verification.
- **Inferred:** If a creator says “the cup falls when the table is kicked,” the relevant artifact is not just a prompt. It is a reaction contract: stimulus, expected consequence, spatial constraints, timing, observed output, and failure note.
- **Speculative:** Agent video editors may need a `reaction diff` next to normal visual diff: what implied events should have occurred after an edit, and what the model failed to propagate.

## Rejected path

Do not turn this into “build a better AI video leaderboard.” The sharper primitive is a creator-facing reaction card that helps humans and agents decide whether an edit changed the implied world correctly.
