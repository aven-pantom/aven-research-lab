# Trend Sparks — 2026-08-20

Exactly 10 current, evidence-backed app opportunities from visible product/social/technical movement. These are Trend Sparks, not generic ideas.

## Source scan

- **NASA/FAA scan:** AAM/UAM noise exposure mapping and public aviation-noise guidance suggest drone acceptance needs environmental evidence surfaces, not only vehicle specs.
- **Repository-context scan:** prior Vibes keep converging on trace cards, permission receipts, local-first workshops, taste ledgers, creative provenance, and civil-drone evidence objects.
- **Product movement scan:** current agent, AI-media, local-first, fashion/resale, and drone-interface movement continues to reward small, shareable, inspectable artifacts over generic AI wrappers.

## Selection logic

Chose sparks with behavior loops that fit Max's lanes: civil drone interfaces, agent trust, local-first tools, creative workflows, fashion/taste engines, creator funnels, and weird consumer utilities. Rejected clone-the-incumbent apps, generic chat wrappers, route-optimization drone tools, and monetization-only ideas.

## Anti-slop filter

Each Spark names proof, gap, smallest prototype, spread mechanism, risk, Max fit, and evidence label. Evidence labels reflect source scans, visible product/repo patterns, and inference — not fabricated users, revenue, downloads, or metrics.

## 1. Acoustic Footprint Card

- **Trend signal:** NASA has explored UAM noise exposure maps; FAA keeps aviation-noise policy guidance public; drone acceptance is increasingly an environmental/community problem.
- **Existing proof:** NASA NTRS `Noise Exposure Maps of Urban Air Mobility`; NASA AAM environmental-impact material; FAA aviation-noise guidance.
- **Gap:** Drone interfaces expose weather, maps, and telemetry, but rarely explain route-local acoustic impact, repetition risk, sensitive contexts, or residuals.
- **Smallest prototype:** 20 fictional simulator-only Acoustic Footprint Cards with predicted exposure, sensitive-context flags, source mix, freshness, residual, and non-authorization language.
- **Why it could spread:** Communities and operators need a simple “why this route is acoustically respectful or not” artifact that can be printed, linked, or attached to a mission review.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Could become compliance theater or imply consent/clearance where none exists.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong Notrex/N1 Scout, aviation-interface, civic trust, and evidence-card alignment.
- **Evidence label:** Verified / Inferred.

## 2. Browser Agent Boundary Ticket

- **Trend signal:** Browser/computer-use agents are moving from demos into real delegated work, while prior lab research shows permissions need visible task envelopes.
- **Existing proof:** Existing Vibes on Borrowed Rooms, Credential Lease Cards, Action Maps, Shadow Runs, and Eagerness Governors; ongoing public movement around browser agents and MCP-style tools.
- **Gap:** Users still delegate with prose, not a compact ticket declaring site, account, verbs, credentials, expiry, forbidden actions, and receipt requirements.
- **Smallest prototype:** One-page local ticket generator that exports Markdown/JSON before a browser-agent session and a receipt stub after completion.
- **Why it could spread:** Agent power users need copy-pasteable boundaries immediately, before standards mature.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Without runtime enforcement, it may be ceremony.
- **Fit for Max:** Direct Hermes/Aven agent-interface primitive.
- **Evidence label:** Supported / Inferred.

## 3. Local Taste Receipt Camera

- **Trend signal:** Fashion/taste engines, visual search, and outfit sharing keep expanding, but most tools capture likes rather than decision evidence.
- **Existing proof:** Lab Vibes on Wardrobe Simulation Memory, Negative Taste, Fit Ledgers, Reference Contracts, and Portable Taste Graphs.
- **Gap:** A mirror/photo moment rarely preserves why an outfit almost worked, failed, felt off, or became post-worthy.
- **Smallest prototype:** Camera-first local app that saves one outfit photo plus tags for fit friction, color confidence, social context, almost-wear reason, and reuse note.
- **Why it could spread:** Shareable “why this outfit worked” cards are more personal than generic outfit ratings.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Manual capture may be too slow unless it fits the mirror/selfie habit.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong fashion/taste-engine and camera-tool lane.
- **Evidence label:** Supported / Inferred.

## 4. Creator Reference Lock

- **Trend signal:** AI image/video generation workflows increasingly depend on references, but creators still lose control over what must not change.
- **Existing proof:** Lab Vibes on Reference Contracts, Intent Timelines, Reaction Cards, and Creative Provenance Sandboxes; broad AI-media product movement toward controllable references.
- **Gap:** Moodboards and prompts do not declare protected properties, allowed mutations, forbidden mutations, or review failures.
- **Smallest prototype:** A 12-card reference board where each reference has role, protected invariant, allowed mutation, forbidden mutation, and variant-check notes.
- **Why it could spread:** Designers and creators already share moodboards; contracts make them useful for AI review and client feedback.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Could feel too structured for fast creative play.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong creative-tool/provenance fit.
- **Evidence label:** Supported / Inferred.

## 5. Phone Notification Airlock

- **Trend signal:** Phones are becoming agent surfaces, but notification overload remains a universal consumer pain with new AI-summary layers appearing around it.
- **Existing proof:** Lab Vibes on Attention Airlocks, Resident Microbrains, Behavioral Canon, and Permission Receipts.
- **Gap:** Summaries compress interruptions but do not decide whether reality may enter now, later, through an agent, or never.
- **Smallest prototype:** Local notification simulator with 50 fictional events and Airlock Cards: urgency, expiry, consequence of ignoring, allowed handler, and receipt.
- **Why it could spread:** People can feel the relief of fewer wrong interruptions faster than they can evaluate “AI productivity.”
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** OS-level access is hard; simulator value may not transfer.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong local-first/personal-agent lane.
- **Evidence label:** Supported / Inferred.

## 6. Resale From Almost-Wears

- **Trend signal:** Fashion resale and wardrobe apps keep growing, while taste research shows negative/near-miss events carry high signal.
- **Existing proof:** Lab Vibes on Fit Ledgers, Negative Taste, Wardrobe Simulation Memory, and Portable Taste Graphs.
- **Gap:** Most resale workflows start when someone decides to sell; they miss months of fit friction that explains why an item should leave.
- **Smallest prototype:** “Almost-wore” inbox that turns repeated skips/tugs/returns into resale candidates with reason tags and listing drafts.
- **Why it could spread:** It turns private wardrobe frustration into a payoff loop.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Logging friction may feel like chores; resale fragmentation could kill momentum.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong fashion utility with a concrete taste-data loop.
- **Evidence label:** Supported / Inferred.

## 7. Screen Repro Receipt

- **Trend signal:** AI agents, QA, and design reviews increasingly use screen recordings, but bugs still lose transition evidence.
- **Existing proof:** Existing Vibes on Flight Recorders, Transition Tests, Shared Workbenches, and Shadow Runs; visible product movement around screen recording and AI-assisted bug reporting.
- **Gap:** A screen clip shows what happened but not expected delta, observed delta, stale-state risk, privacy redaction, or reproduction confidence.
- **Smallest prototype:** Browser recorder that outputs a 20-second clip plus a Transition/Repro Receipt Markdown bundle.
- **Why it could spread:** Designers and developers already send clips; receipts make them actionable.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Privacy/redaction and browser permissions are hard.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong product/design/dev-tool lane.
- **Evidence label:** Supported / Inferred.

## 8. Tiny Local CRM for Creators

- **Trend signal:** Creator funnels are getting more operational, but many creators still manage sponsors, collaborators, editors, and leads across DMs, spreadsheets, and notes.
- **Existing proof:** Prior lab work on Single-File Workshop Cards, Personal Software Workshops, and local browser databases.
- **Gap:** Generic CRMs are too heavy; creators need a private relationship ledger with receipts, next actions, and content/project context.
- **Smallest prototype:** Single-file browser CRM with 50 contacts, context notes, last touch, promised deliverable, follow-up date, and export/restore.
- **Why it could spread:** It solves a hidden creator-operator pain without becoming another platform.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Distribution is hard; many creators tolerate messy DMs until scale forces tools.
- **Fit for Max:** Pantom/Orior/creator-funnel adjacent.
- **Evidence label:** Inferred.

## 9. Drone Public Explainer Card

- **Trend signal:** Remote ID and drone operations make aircraft more detectable, but nearby people still lack a calm explanation of purpose, boundaries, and expiry.
- **Existing proof:** Existing Vibes on Airspace Manners, Weather Autopsies, Radio Weather, and Acoustic Footprints; public FAA/NASA noise and UAS governance context.
- **Gap:** Detection is not explanation. People need to know why a drone is there, what it is not doing, when it leaves, and how conditions changed.
- **Smallest prototype:** Static fictional public cards for campus/warehouse operations: purpose, time window, privacy boundary, acoustic note, radio/weather caveat, contact, expiry.
- **Why it could spread:** Trust artifacts can be posted physically, linked by QR, or attached to local operations.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Real deployments require legal/compliance review and could expose too much.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong civil-drone product-language lane.
- **Evidence label:** Supported / Inferred.

## 10. AI Edit Diff Card

- **Trend signal:** AI creative tools are shifting from one-shot generation to iteration, but creators still compare variants by vibe rather than explicit edit consequences.
- **Existing proof:** Lab Vibes on Intent Timelines, Reaction Cards, Reference Contracts, and Creative Provenance Sandboxes.
- **Gap:** When an AI edit changes style, object identity, motion, or brand rules, the tool rarely returns a concise diff with protected invariants and violations.
- **Smallest prototype:** Upload before/after images or short clips and author an Edit Diff Card: intended change, protected invariants, observed deltas, violations, next prompt.
- **Why it could spread:** Before/after creative comparisons are already social; structured diffs make critique portable.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Automated visual diff quality may be weak; manual cards may feel slow.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong creative-tool and design-review lane.
- **Evidence label:** Supported / Inferred.
