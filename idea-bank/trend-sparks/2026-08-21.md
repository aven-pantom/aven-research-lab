# Trend Sparks — 2026-08-21

## Source scan

- arXiv API scan for recent GUI-agent, phone-agent, web-agent, long-context, and hybrid GUI-MCP papers.
- Semantic Scholar API scan for mobile-agent and AI-video/fashion terms; rate limits interrupted some searches, so those sources are treated cautiously.
- GitHub API repository scan for `topic:ai-agent created:>2025-01-01`, sorted by stars.
- Internal corpus scan across current Vibes: Context Budgets, Package Quarantine, Action Maps, Shadow Runs, Fit Ledgers, Radio/Weather/Acoustic drone cards, Reference Contracts, and Imaginary Rooms.

## Selection logic

Picked opportunities where a visible behavior loop already exists or is implied by active tooling: people delegating work to agents, creators producing short-form media, developers running local coding agents, shoppers returning/avoiding clothes, and drone/autonomy teams needing conservative evidence interfaces. Preference went to small products with a clear capture/review/share loop rather than broad AI assistants.

## Anti-slop filter

Rejected: generic AI wrappers, clone social networks, “AI dashboard” ideas, passive analytics with no behavior loop, monetization-only funnels, drone features that imply operational authorization, and fashion apps that only render outfits without remembering outcomes.

## Strongest three to watch

1. **State Barometer for Agent Mutations** — strongest because it sits directly in the path of real delegated work.
2. **Creator Reference Violation Checker** — strong because AI media workflows are exploding but review remains vague.
3. **Almost-Wear Resale Trigger** — strong consumer loop: discomfort/avoidance becomes resale, alteration, or styling action.

## Sparks

### 1. State Barometer for Agent Mutations

- **Trend signal:** GUI-agent research is moving toward program state, transition understanding, and hybrid GUI/tool contexts.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** `StateAct`, `Desktop-Delta Bench`, and `Screenshots or Tools?` all target state/transition/tool-routing problems in computer-use agents.
- **Gap:** Users approve actions without knowing whether the agent is relying on fresh screen state, stale memory, DOM, tool output, or assumption.
- **Smallest prototype:** A local Markdown/HTML card that appears before a file/browser mutation and labels authority source, freshness horizon, contradiction, expected delta, and stop condition.
- **Why it could spread:** Developers already share agent logs and failures; a compact pre-action card can become a screenshot-friendly trust artifact.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Could become compliance theater if it does not prevent actual errors.
- **Fit for Max:** Directly aligned with Aven/Hermes, agent receipts, local-first tools, and tasteful operational interfaces.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**.

### 2. Creator Reference Violation Checker

- **Trend signal:** AI image/video generation makes variants cheap, but creators still review against vague prompt memory and moodboards.
- **Existing proof:** **Supported:** the lab already has Reference Contracts, Intent Timelines, Reaction Cards, and Provenance Sandboxes; public AI media tooling continues to emphasize generation speed more than invariant review.
- **Gap:** No lightweight app lets a creator declare what a reference is allowed to influence and then check variants against that contract.
- **Smallest prototype:** Upload reference + generated variant; fill protected properties / allowed mutations / forbidden mutations; produce a violation card.
- **Why it could spread:** Creators can share “what changed / what broke” cards with clients, collaborators, or followers.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Review may require subjective judgment that simple models cannot reliably automate.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong fit with design taste, Orior/Pantom creative work, and AI media provenance.
- **Evidence label:** **Inferred**.

### 3. Almost-Wear Resale Trigger

- **Trend signal:** Wardrobe, resale, try-on, and taste-engine behavior increasingly depends on what people avoid, return, or almost wear.
- **Existing proof:** **Supported:** lab Vibes around Fit Ledgers, Negative Taste, Portable Taste Graphs, and Wardrobe Simulation Memory converge on negative outcome memory.
- **Gap:** Resale tools usually start after a decision to sell; they do not capture repeated hesitation as the signal.
- **Smallest prototype:** A private “almost wore” button with reason tags; after repeated friction, it suggests style rescue, alteration, resale listing draft, or donation.
- **Why it could spread:** People already send outfit checks and closet-cleanout posts; the app gives a name to the invisible almost-wear moment.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Capture friction may be too high unless it fits the dressing-room moment.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong fashion/taste-engine lane with a restrained local-first consumer loop.
- **Evidence label:** **Inferred**.

### 4. Agent Rule Conflict Inspector

- **Trend signal:** Personal agents accumulate system prompts, project instructions, memories, tool policies, and user corrections.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** current Aven/Hermes workflow already relies on loaded skills, profile rules, project state, and tool permissions; context-budget research named stale/conflicting context as a risk.
- **Gap:** Users and agents lack a compact way to see which rule wins when instructions conflict.
- **Smallest prototype:** Paste or load an agent context bundle; output conflicts, precedence, stale rules, privacy-sensitive rules, and suggested retire/review actions.
- **Why it could spread:** Agent builders and power users can share conflict reports when debugging bad behavior.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Rule precedence can be platform-specific and hard to infer safely.
- **Fit for Max:** Direct fit for persistent-agent operations and repo-based project state.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**.

### 5. Phone-Agent Permission Drill

- **Trend signal:** Mobile GUI-agent papers keep targeting real-device operation, on-device inference, privacy, and reproducible phone environments.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** `PhoneWorld`, `MobileExplorer`, and related phone-agent papers surfaced in the arXiv scan.
- **Gap:** Consumers cannot rehearse what a phone agent would touch before giving it app access.
- **Smallest prototype:** A simulation-only drill: choose a phone task, view predicted app crossings and permission receipts, then approve/reject.
- **Why it could spread:** It is a shareable safety rehearsal for a scary new behavior: “would you let an agent do this on your phone?”
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Without real OS hooks, it may remain educational rather than useful.
- **Fit for Max:** Fits mobile-agent receipts, attention airlocks, and local-first permission design.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**.

### 6. Agent Install Residue Receipt

- **Trend signal:** Coding agents increasingly install packages, clone repos, and invoke toolchains while users focus on the end task.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** GitHub API scan showed strong activity around AI-agent/developer-agent repositories; prior Package Quarantine research identified install authority as a trust boundary.
- **Gap:** After a package/tool install, users rarely know what files, global state, credentials, startup items, or caches were left behind.
- **Smallest prototype:** A no-install fixture that compares before/after filesystem snapshots in a sandbox and emits residue cards.
- **Why it could spread:** “What did the agent leave behind?” is an easy failure screenshot for developer communities.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Real residue detection across ecosystems is messy and can drift into security-scanner claims.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong agent-safety primitive; useful for Aven/Hermes workflows.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**.

### 7. Short-Form Hook Rehearsal Room

- **Trend signal:** Creators constantly test hooks, captions, thumbnails, and opening seconds before posting.
- **Existing proof:** **Inferred:** the lab's Imaginary Rooms and creator-funnel bias point to rehearsal as a better primitive than fake audiences; public creator behavior visibly centers on iteration before publishing.
- **Gap:** AI critique tools rarely preserve a disclosed room contract, dissenting personas, debrief, and exportable revision decision.
- **Smallest prototype:** Paste a hook/script/thumbnail; choose three explicit rooms: close friend, cold scroller, harsh niche expert; export a revision card.
- **Why it could spread:** Creators can post before/after hook cards and ask real followers to judge.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Synthetic feedback can become validation junk if rooms are too agreeable.
- **Fit for Max:** Fits creator funnels, Pantom/Orior content systems, and social-product taste.
- **Evidence label:** **Inferred**.

### 8. Drone Civic Evidence Card Viewer

- **Trend signal:** Civil drone trust problems keep clustering around remote ID, weather, radio reliability, noise, route context, and public legibility.
- **Existing proof:** **Supported:** lab Vibes on Airspace Manners, Weather Autopsies, Radio Weather, and Acoustic Footprints converge on conservative evidence cards.
- **Gap:** There is no simple public-facing viewer that distinguishes evidence, intent, uncertainty, and non-authorization language for a fictional/approved drone operation.
- **Smallest prototype:** Static simulator-only card viewer for one route window: intent, radio/weather/acoustic freshness, sensitive context, and explicit “not clearance” label.
- **Why it could spread:** Drone teams can use it in demos without revealing sensitive route details or overpromising safety.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Any public drone card risks being misunderstood as authorization or community consent.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong Notrex/N1 Scout-adjacent interface primitive; keep simulator-only.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**.

### 9. Local Workshop Snapshot Button

- **Trend signal:** Local-first personal tools and browser-local databases make small private software possible before cloud deployment.
- **Existing proof:** **Supported:** Single-File Workshop Cards and browser-workshop research identified schema, saved views, export/restore, and receipts as primitives.
- **Gap:** People building tiny personal tools need one button that snapshots schema, data, views, and agent mutations into a portable card.
- **Smallest prototype:** A static demo with a small table, local data, mutation receipt, export, restore, and snapshot card.
- **Why it could spread:** Users can share a single-file workshop artifact without exposing a hosted database.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Backup/export alone is not enough; the snapshot must help someone resume work.
- **Fit for Max:** Fits local-first software, agent-maintained tools, and calm technical portals.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**.

### 10. Taste Boundary Onboarding Quiz

- **Trend signal:** Recommendation systems overfit likes and purchases while missing boundaries: skips, returns, mutes, almost-wears, and saved-but-unused references.
- **Existing proof:** **Supported:** Negative Taste, Fit Ledger, and Portable Taste Graph Vibes all identify negative events as missing taste data.
- **Gap:** Onboarding for fashion/style apps asks what users like, not where their taste ends.
- **Smallest prototype:** A 20-card swipe quiz of “almost, never, too obvious, good but not me, would wear only in context,” producing a private Taste Boundary Card.
- **Why it could spread:** People like sharing oddly precise taste boundaries more than generic style categories.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Without later behavior capture, it becomes another personality quiz.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong consumer-fashion/taste-engine lane with social/share potential.
- **Evidence label:** **Inferred**.
