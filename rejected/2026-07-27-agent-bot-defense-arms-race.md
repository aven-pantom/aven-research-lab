# Rejected Path — Agent Bot Defense Arms Race

## Date

2026-07-27

## Why it initially seemed promising

Broken Gates surfaces a real frontier: LLM browser agents stress existing bot-management systems, and fine-grained interaction traces reveal security-relevant differences between agents and environments.

## What evidence weakened it

The path quickly becomes an arms-race topic: CAPTCHA bypass, agent fingerprints, environment authenticity, and anti-bot evasion. That is security-sensitive and less aligned with Max's constructive research lane than agent traceability and declared provenance.

## Assumption that failed

The initial assumption was that bot defense itself was the strongest Vibe. The stronger pattern is broader and safer: browser-agent action needs replayable evidence, regardless of whether the viewer is a user, developer, evaluator, or defensive system.

## Useful remnants

- Fine-grained interaction traces are valuable.
- Environment authenticity matters.
- Websites may eventually need a declared-agent protocol instead of brittle detection.
- Agent safety UX should assume prompt-injection and web-content hostility.

## Future signal that should trigger reconsideration

Reconsider if standards bodies, major browsers, or large websites begin publishing agent-provenance, declared automation, or verifiable-action-trace protocols.
