## Selection logic

These 10 Trend Sparks were selected because they attach to visible behavior loops: social/video commerce, AI shopping, local/private taste memory, mini-app distribution, agent infrastructure, creator provenance, and drone/aviation legibility. Excluded: generic AI wrappers, generic virtual try-on clones, clone social networks, merchant-only return dashboards, and ideas with no spread loop beyond “AI summarizes it.”

## 10 Trend Sparks

### 1. Fit Ledger

- **Trend signal:** AI virtual try-on, shopping graphs, returns tooling, and social commerce are all active, but garment outcome memory is fragmented.
- **Existing proof:** Google AI virtual try-on, Google Shopping Graph, Shopify returns-management material, TikTok Shop, and active arXiv VTON research are real signals.
- **Gap:** Tools render or recommend clothes without remembering why specific garments failed, were altered, returned, resold, or never worn.
- **Smallest prototype:** A local 30-card Fit Ledger with buy/skip, wear/avoid, and alter/resell events; compare recommendations with and without the ledger.
- **Why it could spread:** “Why I returned / never wore this” is useful, visual, and socially legible; it can travel as a creator artifact without becoming a closet database.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Manual wardrobe tracking often dies unless capture is tied to real decision moments.
- **Fit for Max:** Very strong fashion/taste-engine and local-first fit.
- **Evidence label:** Supported.

### 2. Almost-Wear Capture Button

- **Trend signal:** Fashion creators and shoppers save references, try outfits, and abandon looks, but almost-wears rarely become structured memory.
- **Existing proof:** Prior lab work on Negative Taste Cards and Wardrobe Simulation Memory plus visible social outfit-check behavior supports the loop.
- **Gap:** Style tools over-index likes/saves and miss the boundary where taste becomes “not today.”
- **Smallest prototype:** A phone shortcut or web form that captures a mirror photo, reason not worn, context, and next action in under 30 seconds.
- **Why it could spread:** Almost-wears are emotionally specific and make good private-to-social posts: “the outfit that almost made it.”
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Without a strong visual habit, it becomes journaling homework.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong consumer/taste primitive with a distinct social tone.
- **Evidence label:** Inferred.

### 3. Return Reason Camera

- **Trend signal:** Ecommerce returns remain operationally important while social commerce increases impulse visual buying.
- **Existing proof:** Shopify publishes current returns-management guidance; TikTok Shop and AI try-on increase visual shopping intensity.
- **Gap:** Return workflows collect merchant categories, not user-owned fit/taste memory.
- **Smallest prototype:** A private return capture card: item photo, fit friction, expectation mismatch, keep/return confidence, future avoid rule.
- **Why it could spread:** It turns a frustrating return into a reusable shopping rule and shareable caution artifact.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Retailers may not expose enough structured return data; user capture must be local and fast.
- **Fit for Max:** Good fashion/local-first tool with Orior-style evidence cards.
- **Evidence label:** Supported.

### 4. Creator Outfit Post-Mortem

- **Trend signal:** Creator commerce and outfit videos drive discovery, but audiences rarely see the process constraints behind a final look.
- **Existing proof:** TikTok Shop is a live social-commerce platform; fashion try-on and shopping graphs are mainstreaming visual product discovery.
- **Gap:** Creator posts show the successful outfit, not the failed references, fit constraints, alterations, and decision logic.
- **Smallest prototype:** A share card attached to an outfit post: references, rejected options, fit friction, protected style rules, links.
- **Why it could spread:** Behind-the-scenes decision cards make taste teachable and turn style into a repeatable world.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Creators may prefer polish over exposing failed attempts.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong creator funnel and taste-world fit.
- **Evidence label:** Inferred.

### 5. Mini Shop Critic Room

- **Trend signal:** Farcaster Mini Apps and social mini-app formats normalize interactive tools inside feeds, while shopping decisions often need fast taste feedback.
- **Existing proof:** Farcaster Mini Apps documentation is public; prior lab work on imaginary rooms and taste engines provides the structure.
- **Gap:** Shopping links circulate socially, but critique is scattered across comments, DMs, screenshots, and saved posts.
- **Smallest prototype:** A feed-native mini-app where a user drops one item and gets three disclosed critique roles: fit realist, taste editor, budget/resale skeptic.
- **Why it could spread:** The output is a compact shareable verdict card rather than another chat thread.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Mini-app ecosystems may remain niche or spam-heavy.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong weird social product and fashion taste-engine fit.
- **Evidence label:** Supported.

### 6. Agentic Shopping Permission Preview

- **Trend signal:** Browser/computer-use agents and app intents make delegated shopping technically plausible.
- **Existing proof:** Anthropic computer-use signals, Apple App Intents, MCP, and prior lab Action Maps all point toward machine-operable app surfaces.
- **Gap:** Users need to know which accounts, carts, addresses, payment methods, returns, and messages an agent may touch before it shops.
- **Smallest prototype:** A mock Permission Preview Card for one shopping task: domains, actions, irreversible boundaries, approvals, receipts.
- **Why it could spread:** People are naturally wary of agents near money and accounts; previews make delegation discussable.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Platform browsers may own this layer directly.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong agent trust and consumer utility fit.
- **Evidence label:** Supported.

### 7. Closet Resale Trigger

- **Trend signal:** Fashion resale and returns sit near the same behavior: items that failed, almost worked, or stopped matching a person.
- **Existing proof:** Ecommerce return pressure is documented by Shopify; prior lab Wear Trace/Negative Taste work identifies unworn/returned items as valuable signals.
- **Gap:** Users do not get timely prompts when a Fit Ledger pattern says an item should be resold, tailored, or archived.
- **Smallest prototype:** A local list that flags “worn once / avoided 3 times / similar replacement bought” and drafts a resale note from the ledger.
- **Why it could spread:** It converts wardrobe guilt into a concrete action and shareable listing artifact.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Resale friction may dominate unless integrated with existing marketplaces.
- **Fit for Max:** Good fashion utility with a clear loop.
- **Evidence label:** Inferred.

### 8. Reference Contract for Shopping

- **Trend signal:** AI visual shopping and creator moodboards make references abundant, but product suggestions often mutate the wrong properties.
- **Existing proof:** Google AI try-on and Shopping Graph are live signals; prior lab Reference Contracts provide the creative-control primitive.
- **Gap:** A shopper cannot say “preserve this silhouette, not this color” or “match this mood but avoid this fabric behavior” in a portable way.
- **Smallest prototype:** A shopping reference card with protected properties, allowed mutations, forbidden properties, and fit ledger constraints.
- **Why it could spread:** It makes recommendations feel intentional instead of algorithmic drift.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** People may not want to formalize references unless the UI is visual and fast.
- **Fit for Max:** Very strong bridge between creative tools and taste engines.
- **Evidence label:** Supported.

### 9. Local Agent Fit Scout

- **Trend signal:** Local/resident agents are becoming more plausible, and shopping decisions are full of repeatable personal constraints.
- **Existing proof:** Prior lab Resident Microbrains, Cloudflare Agents, Apple App Intents, and MCP support the agent infrastructure direction.
- **Gap:** Shopping assistants lack a private local memory of fit rules and should not send body/taste data to every retailer.
- **Smallest prototype:** A local agent that reads a Fit Ledger and flags likely fit/taste conflicts on pasted product descriptions.
- **Why it could spread:** It behaves like a quiet personal buyer who says “this will fail for the same reason as the last one.”
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Product pages may omit the material/measurement details needed for useful judgment.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong local-first/personal agent fit.
- **Evidence label:** Inferred.

### 10. Drone Preflight Style Card

- **Trend signal:** Civil drone interfaces need legible preflight artifacts, while consumer apps show that compact cards can make complex decisions understandable.
- **Existing proof:** Prior lab Drone Route Receipts, Air Street Condition Cards, and Action Maps establish the drone legibility lane; this Spark borrows the card grammar from Fit Ledgers.
- **Gap:** Drone builders/operators lack calm, non-operational design artifacts that separate intent, constraints, uncertainty, and post-run outcome.
- **Smallest prototype:** A simulator-only preflight/postflight card for a fictional low-altitude route: intent, weather uncertainty, boundaries, observed outcome, next constraint.
- **Why it could spread:** Builders share logs and cards; the artifact is useful for learning without publishing sensitive routes.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Safety and dual-use constraints require strict simulator-only framing.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong Notrex/N1 Scout interface fit if kept non-operational.
- **Evidence label:** Supported.
