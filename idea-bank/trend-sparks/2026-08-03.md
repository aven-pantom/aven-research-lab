## Anti-slop filter

Rejected patterns:

- Generic MCP marketplace without a trust primitive.
- “AI assistant for X” where the repeated behavior is not specific.
- Direct CapCut, Claude, Grok, Whering, Flightradar, or HabitShare clones.
- Drone apps that imply operational routing/safety authority.
- Fashion apps that only generate outfits and ignore actual wear/reject behavior.

## 1. Capability Receipt Shelf

- **Trend signal:** MCP registries and Apps SDK packaging are turning agent tools into app-like installable capabilities.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** MCP Registry describes itself as “like an app store for MCP servers”; Docker MCP Registry emphasizes signatures, provenance, SBOMs, isolation, and curation; GitHub API showed high attention around MCP server lists and SDKs on 2026-08-03.
- **Gap:** Discovery exists, but users/agents cannot easily compare permission scope, reversibility, real traces, failure modes, and revocation.
- **Smallest prototype:** Local HTML/Markdown shelf for installed MCP tools with permission class, source/version, sample trace, risk note, and revoke command.
- **Why it could spread:** Agent builders need trust UI before normal users let tools mutate accounts, files, payments, or publishing surfaces.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Could become a developer-only config panel unless tied to real install/use moments.
- **Fit for Max:** Very high; strong Hermes/Aven, local-first, agent UX, and receipt-system fit.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**

## 2. Skill Checkout Guard

- **Trend signal:** Agentic payments/skills are appearing in product language.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** Product Hunt RSS surfaced `UniwebPay Skill` as “Financial Infra for the AI era”; OpenAI plugin docs include a Checkout API reference for plugin UI; iTunes showed many high-rating productivity AI agents, including Manus, Claude, and Grok.
- **Gap:** Payment-capable tools need preflight confirmation, spend limits, receipt capture, and reversible/non-reversible labeling.
- **Smallest prototype:** A checkout sandbox wrapper for one fake MCP payment tool: amount cap, merchant identity, approval screen, receipt, cancel path, and incident log.
- **Why it could spread:** The first scary agent moments will involve money; a guardrail that is visible and calm has product pull.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Payment platforms may own this layer directly; legal/compliance complexity is real.
- **Fit for Max:** High; agent permissions, business tooling, and interface trust fit.
- **Evidence label:** **Interesting pattern**

## 3. Local Agent Workbench for Non-Mac Builders

- **Trend signal:** Local coding-agent workbenches are becoming productized.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** Product Hunt RSS surfaced `Termexo` as “A local Windows workbench for Claude Code and Codex.” HN/Algolia surfaced phone/workbench/persistent-computer tools for coding agents in adjacent scans.
- **Gap:** Most agent-dev tooling assumes terminal comfort, macOS/Linux bias, or IDE coupling; Windows/local builders need a calm cockpit with receipts and rollback.
- **Smallest prototype:** Windows-first local web panel: repo picker, agent session, touched files, approve/hold, diff receipt, rollback patch.
- **Why it could spread:** Claude Code/Codex workflows are moving beyond power users; local control and visible state reduce anxiety.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Existing IDEs and vendor apps may absorb the workflow.
- **Fit for Max:** High; Hermes operations and productized agent-control surface.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**

## 4. Voice Expense Memory With Proof

- **Trend signal:** Voice-first money capture is appearing in product feeds.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** Product Hunt RSS surfaced `Finamie` as “Speak your expenses and get instant spending insights.” iTunes query for AI agents/productivity shows large demand for assistant-like utilities.
- **Gap:** Expense apps capture numbers, but not contextual proof: why it was bought, category uncertainty, receipt link, reimbursement risk, recurring pattern, and business/personal boundary.
- **Smallest prototype:** Voice expense note that creates one proof card: amount, merchant, why, category confidence, attachment slot, reimbursement/tax flag, monthly pattern.
- **Why it could spread:** Speaking an expense is faster than categorizing it; proof cards make the habit useful later.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Finance apps already fight for this space; accuracy/trust must be high.
- **Fit for Max:** Medium-high; personal software and small-business operating tools fit.
- **Evidence label:** **Interesting pattern**

## 5. Wear/Rewear Decision Queue

- **Trend signal:** Closet and outfit-planning apps remain visibly active mobile categories.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** iTunes Search returned Whering (10,982 ratings), Fits (4,866), Combyne (87,045), Alta Daily (11,631), Style DNA (7,384), and Closetly (1,880) in outfit/closet queries.
- **Gap:** Most wardrobe tools plan outfits, but do not turn almost-wears and repeated skips into keep/alter/sell/rewear decisions.
- **Smallest prototype:** Local morning queue: log “almost wore,” reason, competing item, weather/context, rewear/sell/tailor action.
- **Why it could spread:** It converts daily closet hesitation into a useful feed and resale/tailoring loop.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Manual wardrobe logging is fragile unless capture is almost frictionless.
- **Fit for Max:** Very high; fashion/taste engine and negative-space preference memory.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**

## 6. Wrong-But-Close Moodboard

- **Trend signal:** Moodboard apps have durable demand while creators need better taste communication with AI tools and collaborators.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** iTunes Search returned Moodboard (1,709 ratings), Morpholio Board (3,750), Milanote (3,553), and Vision Board & Goal Tracker (3,756).
- **Gap:** Boards collect inspiration but rarely preserve what is close-but-wrong: too sterile, too loud, too brand-like, too safe, too derivative.
- **Smallest prototype:** Drop 20 images; mark five as wrong-but-close; export a positive/negative Taste Boundary Card for agents/designers.
- **Why it could spread:** “Do not make it like this” is a repeated creative behavior and shareable artifact.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Without strong extraction, it becomes another pretty board.
- **Fit for Max:** Very high; design systems, taste engines, Orior/Pantom creative direction.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**

## 7. Flight-Day Anxiety Card

- **Trend signal:** Flight tracking remains a mainstream utility ritual.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** iTunes Search returned Flightradar24 (647,580 ratings), FlightAware (378,945), Flight Tracker + (289,193), and Flightview (6,340).
- **Gap:** Trackers show aircraft; travelers need a personal card: inbound aircraft, delay risk, gate churn, bag timing, pickup coordination, and next useful action.
- **Smallest prototype:** Forward itinerary email; generate a live-link trip card with only what matters next.
- **Why it could spread:** Travelers repeatedly check scattered sources; a calm shareable card reduces anxiety.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Airline apps and incumbents own key data; live reliability may be hard.
- **Fit for Max:** High; aviation UX and personal software fit.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**

## 8. Drone Neighbor Explainer

- **Trend signal:** Drone apps and airspace-safety utilities are present, but bystander understanding remains weak.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** iTunes Search returned FAA `B4UFLY Drone Airspace Safety` (499 ratings), Drone Buddy (8,422), DJI Fly (4,527), and drone simulator/control-adjacent apps.
- **Gap:** Existing tools help pilots more than neighbors; a bystander needs non-operational education on what can/cannot be inferred and what response is appropriate.
- **Smallest prototype:** Educational card: “A drone may be nearby; here is what Remote ID/airspace tools can mean, what they cannot mean, and when to ignore/report.”
- **Why it could spread:** Public drone anxiety will rise with civil autonomy; legibility is a social primitive.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Must avoid tactical tracking, doxxing, or safety-sensitive claims.
- **Fit for Max:** High; drones, aviation interfaces, civil autonomy manners.
- **Evidence label:** **Interesting pattern**

## 9. Screen Recording to Procedure Card

- **Trend signal:** Screen recording is a high-demand utility and agent workflows need executable procedure memory.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** iTunes Search returned Record it! (450,624 ratings), Go Record (110,569), and other screen recorders with large rating counts; HN/Algolia surfaced screen/workflow agent tooling.
- **Gap:** Recordings are heavy; agents need distilled procedures with timestamps, exceptions, approval gates, and replay bounds.
- **Smallest prototype:** Record 60 seconds; generate a Markdown Procedure Card with trigger, steps, exceptions, source timestamps, and confidence labels.
- **Why it could spread:** Loom-style training already exists; making it agent-readable is a natural next behavior.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Extraction can hallucinate hidden steps unless user-reviewed and timestamp-grounded.
- **Fit for Max:** Very high; Hermes skills, local-first agents, creator operations.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**

## 10. Friend Habit Proof Ring

- **Trend signal:** Social habit trackers continue to package accountability into small-group behavior.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** iTunes Search returned HabitShare (653 ratings), HabitLink (314), Habit Tracker (144,776), Habitify (7,044), and Shared Habit Tracker: Habitomo.
- **Gap:** Streaks are shallow; small groups need proof artifacts, friction notes, witness reactions, and weekly recap identity.
- **Smallest prototype:** Private 7-day ring: daily proof card, one friction note, one witness reaction, and a weekly shareable recap.
- **Why it could spread:** Small private groups can create social pressure without requiring a large network.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Habit apps often decay quickly; the identity/status hook must be real.
- **Fit for Max:** Medium-high; social product loop and creator/content funnel adjacency.
- **Evidence label:** **Interesting pattern**

2. **Wrong-But-Close Moodboard** — strongest creative/taste product loop; precise, shareable, and Max-aligned.
