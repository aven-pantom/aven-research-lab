# Experiment — Fit Ledger Card

## Purpose
Test whether lightweight fit/outcome memory improves fashion recommendations more than visual references or size-chart thinking alone.

## Hypothesis
A recommendation brief that includes negative fit events, returns, alterations, and almost-wears will produce more specific and useful shopping/style decisions than a brief built only from positive references and desired aesthetics.

## Materials
- 30 hand-authored Fit Ledger Cards:
  - 10 kept and frequently worn
  - 10 returned, resold, or avoided
  - 10 almost-worn or altered
- One shopping task, e.g. “find a light jacket for warm evenings that works with black trousers and does not feel too formal.”
- Two recommendation prompts:
  - A: positive references and desired aesthetic only
  - B: same references plus Fit Ledger memory

## Card fields
- garment
- brand / size / material
- acquisition source
- intended context
- fit friction
- negative taste reason
- outcome
- alteration / return / resale action
- compatibility notes
- confidence

## Procedure
1. Create the 30-card fixture as Markdown or JSON.
2. Generate recommendation brief A from positive references only.
3. Generate recommendation brief B from the full ledger.
4. Compare outputs for specificity, avoided mistakes, body/context sensitivity, and actionability.
5. Record which ledger fields changed the recommendation.

## Success criteria
- Brief B names constraints that Brief A misses.
- Brief B avoids at least three plausible bad recommendations using negative evidence.
- The card grammar feels capturable in under 60 seconds at buy/return/wear moments.

## Failure criteria
- Ledger fields produce generic advice only.
- Manual capture feels heavier than the value produced.
- Positive references alone are enough for the same decision quality.

## Stopping condition
Stop after one 30-card fixture and one shopping task. Do not build a closet app until the ledger changes recommendations in a visible way.
