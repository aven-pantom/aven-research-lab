# Unexpected Connection — Resident Microbrains and Night Watches

## Connection

Resident local agents resemble night watches more than assistants.

A night watch is not the smartest person in the institution. It has a narrow duty: stay present, notice specific conditions, avoid unnecessary disturbance, wake the right person when the threshold is crossed, and leave a log.

A resident microbrain has the same shape.

## Shared structure

- **Continuous presence:** Both systems matter because they are there when the main actor is absent.
- **Narrow authority:** They should not solve every problem; they should classify and escalate.
- **Wake conditions:** Value depends on knowing when silence is correct and when interruption is required.
- **Receipts:** The log is the trust object. Without it, silence could mean nothing happened, something was missed, or something was hidden.
- **Low-cost vigilance:** The system must be cheap enough to run constantly without exhausting the host.

## Transferable mechanism

Design resident agents like duty rosters:

- duty scope
- watch hours
- observed surfaces
- forbidden zones
- wake thresholds
- escalation target
- handoff note
- missed-event review
- revocation

## Why it matters

Most agent interfaces frame always-on AI as a more convenient chatbot. The night-watch analogy forces a better question: what should this agent be trusted to notice while doing almost nothing?

That shifts the product from personality to operational doctrine.

## Evidence label

**Inferred:** The analogy is structural, derived from verified signals around local always-on models, mobile duty-agent constraints, and small-model security risks.
