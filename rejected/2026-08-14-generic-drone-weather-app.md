# Rejected — Generic Drone Weather App

## Why it initially seemed promising

Weather is a real blocker for civil drones, and operators need better low-altitude context than airport-level forecasts. A drone-specific weather app sounds immediately useful.

## What weakened it

- Weather dashboards and aviation weather sources already exist.
- A generic app would likely become another preflight panel rather than a learning system.
- It risks overclaiming route safety from sparse or non-local data.
- It does not capture what the aircraft actually experienced.
- It could drift toward live operational guidance before the evidence model is mature.

## Failed assumption

The weak assumption was: “better drone weather means better forecast UI.” The stronger assumption is: “better drone weather begins with structured residual memory after flight.”

## Useful remnants

- Forecast snapshots still matter as the expected-state baseline.
- Weather-risk language and freshness labels are necessary.
- Operator-facing presentation should be calm and conservative.

## Future signal that should trigger reconsideration

Reconsider a weather interface when Weather Autopsy Cards show that post-flight residuals reliably change planning decisions, or when insurers/fleet operators need a reviewable evidence trail for aborted or degraded missions.
