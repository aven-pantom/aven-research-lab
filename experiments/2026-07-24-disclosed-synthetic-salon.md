# Experiment Spec — Disclosed Synthetic Salon

## Question

Can disclosed synthetic inhabitants make a narrow social product feel alive without making users feel deceived or replaced?

## Hypothesis

A role-bound, visibly synthetic salon will increase first-session posting and reply behavior compared with an empty room, while preserving trust if users can hide and inspect the synthetic layer.

## Setup

- Domain: taste/fashion/visual design salon.
- Input: one posted artifact: outfit photo, room photo, color palette, song link, or design image.
- Synthetic inhabitants: 5–9 role-bound agents.
- Human participants: small private test group.

## Conditions

1. Empty room.
2. Synthetic salon with labels and hide toggle.
3. Synthetic salon plus visible culture trace.

## Measurements

- time to first post,
- replies per human post,
- human-to-human replies after synthetic replies,
- hide-synthetic toggle usage,
- trust rating,
- cringe/spam reports,
- invitation intent,
- qualitative descriptions of the inhabitants.

## Success criteria

- users understand synthetic roles within 30 seconds,
- synthetic replies increase human posting or responding,
- users do not describe the room as fake engagement,
- at least one human-to-human exchange is catalyzed by a synthetic response,
- culture trace is perceived as useful rather than surveillance-like.

## Failure criteria

- users feel tricked,
- synthetic inhabitants dominate the room,
- users consume responses but do not participate,
- agent replies become repetitive praise,
- users would not invite a friend.

## Stopping condition

Stop if the synthetic layer consistently reduces desire to invite real humans. That means the prototype is an entertainment toy, not social scaffolding.
