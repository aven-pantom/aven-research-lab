# Unexpected Connection — Video Compilers and CAD Design History

## Connection

AI video editing and parametric CAD are starting to expose the same hidden structure: the final artifact is less valuable when detached from the sequence of intentions that produced it.

## Shared hidden problem

Both domains suffer from flattened exports:

- A rendered video hides prompts, references, timing decisions, rejected takes, manual edits, approvals, and export settings.
- A mesh or static CAD file hides sketches, constraints, parameters, feature order, failed branches, and fabrication intent.

In both cases, later revision becomes archaeology unless the tool preserves a replayable history.

## Transferable mechanism

CAD history suggests a useful video primitive: an **Intent Timeline Card** that records editable dependencies rather than only pixels. For video, the history needs to include model prompts, source assets, motion parameters, shot structure, manual overrides, review notes, and replay confidence.

## Why this matters

If AI video tools only optimize generation speed, creators get more outputs but less control. If they become history-native, creators get reusable style systems, client-review diffs, provenance before publication, and agent-editable project memory.

## Evidence labels

- **Verified:** Current scans surfaced AI-native video editors, editable prompt-to-motion tools, video-as-code language experiments, and CapCut/short-drama app visibility.
- **Supported:** Prior lab research already identified CAD design history, creative provenance, and agent flight recorders as trace-native primitives.
- **Inferred:** The same trace-native logic can transfer from CAD/software/agent systems into AI video editing.
