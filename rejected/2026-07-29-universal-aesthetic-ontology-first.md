# Rejected Path — Universal Aesthetic Ontology First

## Date

2026-07-29

## Why it initially seemed promising

A portable taste graph seems to imply a universal vocabulary for style, mood, color, media, creators, and shopping. A common ontology would make cross-app taste queries cleaner.

## What evidence weakened it

The scanned sources support portable records and interpreters more than shared aesthetic meaning. AT Protocol repositories, custom feeds, ActivityPub objects, Solid resources, and Pinterest pins/boards show structured artifacts and decentralized interpretation, not one universal taxonomy.

## Which assumption failed

The failed assumption: interoperability requires shared aesthetic semantics first.

A better assumption: interoperability may only require shared event/provenance/context records. Meaning can remain interpreter-specific.

## Whether it may become viable later

Viable later if many taste-card tools converge on repeated tags and negative-signal structures. The ontology should be extracted from use, not designed up front.

## Useful remnants

- Keep `aesthetic_tags.user_words` separate from `aesthetic_tags.model_words`.
- Keep `negative_reason` as a first-class field.
- Let apps publish interpreters rather than claiming universal taste truth.

## Future signal for reconsideration

Three or more independent apps/protocols adopt overlapping style/taste schemas, or a creator/community standard emerges around portable moodboards, wardrobe traces, or preference events.

## Evidence label

**Inferred** from verified protocol sources and the absence of a complete cross-domain taste schema in the scanned material.
