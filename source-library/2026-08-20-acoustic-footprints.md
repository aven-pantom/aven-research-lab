# Source Notes — Acoustic Footprints for Civil Drones

## Signal

Low-altitude civil aircraft are usually discussed through route safety, weather, detect-and-avoid, Remote ID, and connectivity. A separate source cluster says noise is not a late community-relations issue; it is an environmental field that must be predicted, mapped, perceived by humans, explained, and remembered.

## Sources checked

### NASA NTRS — `Noise Exposure Maps of Urban Air Mobility`

- **URL:** https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20210017028
- **Type:** Conference paper, NASA Ames / USRA, public.
- **What it provides:** **Verified:** The paper explicitly explores applying airport-style noise exposure maps to Urban Air Mobility. It uses vertiport locations, UAM routes from NASA engineering simulations, local noise-sensitive facilities, land-use information, and NASA AIRNOISEUAM predictions for a six-passenger electric quadrotor prototype in the Dallas-Fort Worth area.
- **Why it matters:** It treats UAM noise as geographic evidence, not just as a vehicle acoustic property. This supports a map/card primitive, but it is still closer to planning contours than operator/community-facing route evidence.
- **Constraint:** The evidence is simulated/planning-oriented; it does not prove everyday community acceptance or real small-drone deployment.

### NASA NTRS — `Advanced Air Mobility: Environmental Impact (Noise and Emissions)`

- **URL:** https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230012372
- **Type:** NASA Langley presentation at ICAS Emerging Technology Forum.
- **What it provides:** **Verified:** NASA frames AAM environmental impact around noise and emissions, showing that noise remains a first-order concern for AAM integration rather than an afterthought.
- **Why it matters:** It supports the timing: AAM/civil autonomy cannot be evaluated only through flight feasibility and airspace integration.
- **Constraint:** Presentation evidence is less detailed than a paper; use it as context, not as proof of a specific interface primitive.

### FAA — Aviation Noise policy guidance

- **URL:** https://www.faa.gov/regulations_policies/policy_guidance/noise
- **Type:** FAA public guidance hub.
- **What it provides:** **Verified:** FAA maintains a public aviation-noise policy/guidance surface. Aviation noise is already a regulatory/public policy domain, not merely an engineering variable.
- **Why it matters:** Any civil-drone acoustic primitive must be conservative, non-authorizing, and compatible with policy language.
- **Constraint:** Current FAA noise frameworks were built around conventional aviation/airports more than dense low-altitude drone presence.

### Prior lab source base

- **Radio Weather / Weather Autopsies / Airspace Manners:** Existing Vibes already represent low-altitude drone conditions as evidence cards with freshness, residuals, privacy redaction, and conservative review language.
- **Why it matters:** Acoustic Footprint Cards can reuse the same card grammar while adding human perception, local norms, time-of-day, and cumulative exposure.

## Existing evidence pattern

- **Verified:** NASA has already explored UAM noise exposure mapping with simulated routes, vertiports, sensitive facilities, land use, and AIRNOISEUAM.
- **Verified:** NASA and FAA treat aviation/AAM noise as a serious environmental/community-integration domain.
- **Supported:** Noise is spatial, temporal, perceptual, and political; a route can be technically safe but socially unacceptable.
- **Inferred:** Civil drone interfaces need acoustic evidence closer to a route condition card than a one-time vehicle certification number.

## Contradictions and uncertainty

- **Verified:** Current source evidence is stronger for planning maps and environmental assessment than for real-time operator UX.
- **Supported:** Noise annoyance depends on psychoacoustic qualities, background context, repetition, time-of-day, expectation, and trust; decibels alone are insufficient.
- **Inferred:** Overly polished acoustic cards could create false legitimacy: “quiet enough” language may be socially or legally unsafe if not backed by standards.
- **Unknown:** The minimum variables that communities, operators, and regulators would all find useful are not established.

## Useful terms

- Noise exposure map
- Noise-sensitive facilities
- Day-night average sound level / DNL
- AAM environmental impact
- Acoustic signature

- Community annoyance
- Cumulative exposure

## Research implication

The missing primitive is not a quieter rotor or a generic drone-noise dashboard. It is a conservative **Acoustic Footprint Card**: predicted and observed acoustic impact for a route class or operation window, with source mix, freshness, sensitive-context flags, residuals, and explicit non-authorization language.
