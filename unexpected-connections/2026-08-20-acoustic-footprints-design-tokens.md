# Unexpected Connection — Acoustic Footprints and Design Tokens

## Connection

Civil drone noise maps and design-system tokens look unrelated. One describes sound exposure in cities; the other describes interface styling. The shared structure is constraint inheritance.

A design token is not just a color value. It is a portable promise: this surface uses this role, under these conditions, so future changes can preserve the system's character. An acoustic footprint could work the same way: this route segment has this acoustic role, under this time window and local context, so future operations can preserve community quiet instead of treating every flight as independent.

## Shared hidden problem

Both fields struggle when raw measurements replace named constraints.

- In design, raw hex values do not preserve intent, hierarchy, or atmosphere.
- In drone acoustics, raw decibels do not preserve time-of-day, repetition, sensitive places, background contrast, or local tolerance.

## Transferable mechanism

Use role-based constraint cards:

- protected quiet zone,
- short-duration transit corridor,
- maintenance/noise-tolerant window,
- sensitive recovery area,
- school/clinic sleep or care boundary,
- repeated-exposure warning.

Each role would carry allowed operations, forbidden simplifications, expiry, evidence source, and residual notes.

## Why it matters

If low-altitude autonomy only optimizes numeric cost fields, it will learn to route around regulations while still violating atmosphere. Acoustic Footprint Cards should preserve local acoustic intent the way good design tokens preserve visual intent.

**Inferred:** NASA/FAA sources verify the seriousness of UAM/aviation noise and NASA's exposure-map approach. The design-token analogy is a structural transfer, not a sourced claim.
