## Selection logic

Chose sparks with a real behavior loop, visible source evidence, and fit with Max's frontier bias: agents, creative tools, local-first software, fashion/taste, drones/aviation interfaces, and weird social/consumer utilities. Prioritized sparks that could start as cards, fixtures, local tools, or small social loops rather than full platforms.

## Anti-slop filter

Rejected generic AI wrappers, clone-the-incumbent apps, prompt-only dashboards, pure monetization tactics, operational drone-routing tools, and ideas whose only evidence was “AI is trending.” Each spark has a smallest prototype and a reason it could spread through behavior, not just novelty.

## 1. Playable Benchmark Card

- **Trend signal:** `PlayWorld` introduces Agent Players for long-horizon world-model objectives; current world models fail spatial consistency and persistent state evolution under interaction.
- **Existing proof:** arXiv:2608.13552v1; related current papers AirForesight, HumanTracker, V-RAE, and AutoDesign all challenge passive/static metrics.
- **Gap:** Creative/world-model tools still review outputs as clips, images, or scores instead of objective-driven failure traces.
- **Smallest prototype:** Hand-author 12 Playable Benchmark Cards across AI video scenes, simulator-only UAV route imagination, humanoid contact, and design artifacts.
- **Why it could spread:** Creators and researchers can share “watch it break here” receipts instead of arguing about vibes or scores.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Agent-player benchmarks may evaluate the player more than the model; casual creators may not want structured QA.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong creative-tool, autonomy, agent-trace, and Notrex/Pantom-adjacent research primitive.
- **Evidence label:** Verified / Inferred.

## 2. Agent Leadership Board

- **Trend signal:** HN surfaced “Working with AI Feels More Like Leadership Than Coding” and “Auto-research with Codex,” while GitHub shows active agent workflow/skills repositories.
- **Existing proof:** HN posts and current GitHub repositories like `the-sarge/skills`, `agent-commander`, and local agent workflow projects.
- **Gap:** Agent work is managed through chats and terminals, not leadership-style boards showing delegation, blocked decisions, review debt, and morale-free accountability.
- **Smallest prototype:** A local board for three agent tasks: objective, authority, current artifact, open decisions, review debt, stop condition, and replay link.
- **Why it could spread:** Builders running multiple coding/research agents immediately need an operating surface beyond tabs and transcripts.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Existing IDEs/agent clients may absorb it; too much management UI can slow solo builders.
- **Fit for Max:** Direct Aven/Hermes operations fit; extends Shared Workbenches, Continuation Ports, and Eagerness Governors.
- **Evidence label:** Supported / Inferred.

## 3. Line-Art Motion Instrument

- **Trend signal:** HN surfaced Disney Research's 2D Gaussian Splatting for Bézier Spline Line Art Vectorization alongside interactive creative toys like Eigendrum.
- **Existing proof:** Disney Research public post on 2D Gaussian Splatting for Bézier line-art vectorization; HN item for Eigendrum.
- **Gap:** Vectorization tools convert drawings but rarely turn line structure into editable motion/material behavior.
- **Smallest prototype:** Upload a sketch, convert/trace strokes into spline groups, assign motion rules, export a short animated SVG/Lottie plus a Motion Receipt.
- **Why it could spread:** Artists love seeing their own marks become alive without learning animation software.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** High-quality vectorization is hard; artists may prefer existing Procreate/After Effects workflows.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong creative-tool and design-language lane; connects Intent Timelines and Reaction Cards.
- **Evidence label:** Verified / Inferred.

## 4. Local-First Tiny Ledger

- **Trend signal:** GitHub current scan shows local-first apps and databases remain active: `actualbudget/actual`, `zorn/local_cents`, `BriskDB`, and `tidebreak`.
- **Existing proof:** HN surfaced BriskDB; GitHub search surfaced active local-first finance and agentic coworker repos.
- **Gap:** Local-first remains developer/finance coded; ordinary personal tools need tiny ledgers with schema, receipts, export, and agent-safe verbs.
- **Smallest prototype:** Single-file browser ledger for one domain: wardrobe wears, research sources, creator invoices, or app ideas, with export/restore and mutation receipts.
- **Why it could spread:** People trust small private ledgers more than cloud dashboards when the data is personal but useful.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Sync/backups become hard quickly; users may not value custody until something breaks.
- **Fit for Max:** Direct Single-File Workshop Card and local-first personal software lane.
- **Evidence label:** Supported / Inferred.

## 5. Try-On Friction Diary

- **Trend signal:** GitHub scan shows many virtual try-on/fashion AI repos, and prior lab work already found fit memory sharper than generic visual try-on.
- **Existing proof:** Current repos including `VirtualFit_AI`, `Bootique-Design-Studio`, and `shop-tryon-skill` advertise AI/AR try-on flows.
- **Gap:** Try-on tools render the image but do not remember friction: tugging, sleeve behavior, returns, almost-wears, alteration notes, resale intent.
- **Smallest prototype:** Mobile diary for 30 garments with photo, fit friction tags, context, kept/returned/avoided outcome, and next recommendation note.
- **Why it could spread:** Fashion users already screenshot/ask friends; remembering failures creates a sharper personal advantage.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Manual logging may be too much; ecommerce platforms may own post-purchase loops.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong fashion/taste-engine lane; extends Fit Ledger and Negative Taste Cards.
- **Evidence label:** Supported / Inferred.

## 6. Drone Mission Explainability Deck

- **Trend signal:** Current arXiv UAV papers emphasize future spatial reasoning; GitHub scan surfaced active GCS/mission planning repos like `ardudeck`, `ADOSMissionControl`, and `FICSAgenticDroneSim`.
- **Existing proof:** AirForesight arXiv:2608.12835v1; active open-source drone planning/simulation repositories.
- **Gap:** Ground-control tools show missions and parameters, but not compact explanation cards for why a simulated autonomous plan changed.
- **Smallest prototype:** Simulator-only Mission Explanation Deck: objective, assumed map, future-map uncertainty, waypoint rationale, forbidden action, and safety note.
- **Why it could spread:** Civil drone teams need legibility for autonomy without exposing sensitive routes or pretending to authorize flight.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Safety/regulatory complexity may make lightweight cards insufficient; must avoid operational guidance.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong N1 Scout/Notrex-adjacent but kept conservative and simulator-only.
- **Evidence label:** Verified / Inferred.

## 7. Unicode Ghost Inspector

- **Trend signal:** HN surfaced “A Spectre Is Haunting Unicode,” showing renewed attention to invisible/ambiguous text behavior.
- **Existing proof:** Public technical article and HN discussion; recurring security/dev pain around invisible characters, homoglyphs, and copy-paste ambiguity.
- **Gap:** Most inspectors are developer utilities, not calm everyday tools for writers, designers, and agents passing text between systems.
- **Smallest prototype:** Paste/share text into a tiny inspector that shows invisible characters, confusables, directionality marks, and safe-clean copy with a receipt.
- **Why it could spread:** “Why does this text behave weird?” is instantly shareable and useful across dev, design, and content workflows.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Browser/IDE extensions may already satisfy developers; mainstream users may not care often enough.
- **Fit for Max:** Weird consumer utility plus agent-safety tool; useful for Pantom/Orior publishing workflows.
- **Evidence label:** Verified / Inferred.

## 8. Search Spike Studio Room

- **Trend signal:** Google Trends current RSS shows fast fandom/news/sports spikes; social platforms monetize attention but do not give creators a private production room around a spike.
- **Existing proof:** Trends RSS items included entertainment, sports, and current-event searches during the run.
- **Gap:** Creators get trending topics but not a small room that converts a spike into references, angles, wardrobe/mood, clip hooks, and expiration.
- **Smallest prototype:** 24-hour trend room: pick a spike, collect references, generate five content angles, assign taste constraints, export a post brief.
- **Why it could spread:** Time-boxed trend rooms match how creators already chase attention, but make the workflow more intentional.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Trend chasing can become slop; rights/moderation/news sensitivity is high.
- **Fit for Max:** Creator funnel + social product + taste-world lane if kept restrained and not engagement-farm coded.
- **Evidence label:** Verified / Inferred.

## 9. Personal AI Network Policy Card

- **Trend signal:** `Rethinking Agent Security as a Networking Problem` argues agent security should borrow networking principles: centralized control, distributed enforcement, capability access, zero trust, plus semantic policy.
- **Existing proof:** arXiv:2608.12172v1; GitHub scan shows more local personal-AI and browser-agent projects.
- **Gap:** Personal AI permissions are scattered across app settings, API keys, browser sessions, and prompts.
- **Smallest prototype:** A local Policy Card for one agent task: resources, allowed flows, blocked flows, semantic exceptions, expiry, and enforcement receipt.
- **Why it could spread:** Anyone granting agents tool access needs a visible “network policy for my work” without reading security docs.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** May be too technical for consumers; platforms may lock enforcement below app level.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong Credential Lease, Room Contract, Action Map, and local-first trust UX fit.
- **Evidence label:** Verified / Inferred.

## 10. Shape-to-Sound Microtoy

- **Trend signal:** HN surfaced Eigendrum, a small web toy where users draw any shape and hear it as a drum.
- **Existing proof:** Public Eigendrum demo link appeared in current HN top stories.
- **Gap:** The playful interaction is strong, but there is room for a broader class of tiny sensory translators: shape to sound, motion to rhythm, outfit to chord, route to ambience.
- **Smallest prototype:** One-page toy: draw/import a shape, hear it, mutate with three constraints, export a shareable loop/card.
- **Why it could spread:** Weird sensory toys travel because the output is personal, surprising, and instantly demonstrable.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Novelty may be short-lived; audio quality matters quickly.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong weird consumer/creative instrument lane; low-cost content funnel experiment.
- **Evidence label:** Verified / Inferred.
