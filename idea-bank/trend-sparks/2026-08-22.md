# Trend Sparks — 2026-08-22

## Source scan

- arXiv API scan for recent computer-use agents, mobile agents, AI video editing, video virtual try-on, local/edge models, and drone/UAM papers.
- GitHub API scans for `computer use agent`, `mcp server`, `virtual try on`, `ai video editing`, and `local first app`, filtered to repositories created after 2025-01-01 and sorted by stars.
- Internal corpus scan across Task Models, State Barometers, Action Maps, Fit Ledgers, Reference Contracts, Weather/Radio/Acoustic drone cards, Imaginary Rooms, and Personal Software Workshops.

## Selection logic

Picked opportunities with visible technical or behavioral proof: computer-use traces becoming structured models, component-level GUI benchmarks, mobile-agent safety work, rapidly active MCP/tool surfaces, multi-shot AI video editing, video virtual try-on, and UAM/drone evidence work. Preference went to small products with a capture/review/share loop and a Max-aligned frontier.

## Anti-slop filter

Rejected: generic agent dashboards, “record and automate anything” macros, prompt-wrapper video editors, basic outfit generators, drone tools that imply operational clearance, and trend ideas without an existing proof signal.

## Strongest three to watch

1. **Task Model Card** — strongest because it converts agent traces into reusable procedure knowledge without immediately granting automation.
2. **Component Failure Replay Deck** — strong developer wedge for computer-use agents because benchmarks are moving into the under-instrumented middle layer.
3. **Reference Drift Ledger for AI Video** — strong creative-tool wedge because multi-shot editing and video try-on both fight identity/reference drift.

## Sparks

### 1. Task Model Card

- **Trend signal:** Computer-use-agent research is moving from replay and summaries toward symbolic, auditable, reusable task models induced from naturalistic traces.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** `Inducing Task Models from Computer-Use Traces` was published 2026-08-20 and explicitly targets task model induction from passively recorded screenshots and actions.
- **Gap:** Users have traces, logs, and memories, but no compact artifact that says what the agent thinks the recurring procedure is and where it must stop.
- **Smallest prototype:** A local Markdown/JSON card for five safe workflows with objective tree, procedure flow, state checks, forbidden transitions, expiry triggers, and source trace link.
- **Why it could spread:** Agent builders already share traces and failures; “turn this trace into a reviewable procedure” is a clearer loop than another chat memory feature.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** It could collapse into old RPA/macro recording if boundaries and expiry are weak.
- **Fit for Max:** Direct fit for Aven/Hermes, local-first tools, personal software workshops, and agent receipts.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**.

### 2. Component Failure Replay Deck

- **Trend signal:** GUI-agent evaluation is filling the middle layer between atomic click grounding and long workflow success.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** `ComponentBench` reports a component ontology with 97 canonical UI components and 2,910 programmatically verified tasks.
- **Gap:** Agent developers often see “task failed” without knowing which component family, interaction burden, or structural difficulty caused the failure.
- **Smallest prototype:** Static replay cards for 20 UI-component failures: component type, expected interaction, observed action, structural difficulty, recovery path, and replay snippet.
- **Why it could spread:** Computer-use-agent teams need screenshot-friendly failure artifacts for debugging and benchmark discussions.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Component taxonomies may not transfer cleanly across real product UIs.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong fit for agent observability, transition tests, and design-level interface critique.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**.

### 3. Mobile Injection Rehearsal Room

- **Trend signal:** Mobile GUI agents are approaching real app use while environmental content can manipulate them through indirect instructions.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** `MobileWorldSafety` introduces 142 risk tasks on real Android applications with final-state risk indicators.
- **Gap:** Consumers and builders lack a safe way to rehearse “what could trick this phone agent?” before connecting real accounts.
- **Smallest prototype:** Simulation-only cards for common phone tasks showing environmental text, intended user task, forbidden action, final-state risk indicator, and safe response.
- **Why it could spread:** It creates a visceral share loop: “would your phone agent fall for this screen?”
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Without real OS hooks, it may remain an educational game rather than protection.
- **Fit for Max:** Fits permission receipts, attention airlocks, mobile agents, and safety UX.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**.

### 4. Intention Trace Coach

- **Trend signal:** Active mobile-agent research is using continuous action histories to infer user intentions rather than waiting for explicit commands.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** `Act2Intention` reports 72,511 intentions and more than 700,000 actions across 52 apps.
- **Gap:** Proactive agents may infer intent without showing the user what pattern triggered the inference or how to correct it.
- **Smallest prototype:** A private local card that maps a short action sequence to guessed intention, confidence reason, do-not-infer boundary, and correction button.
- **Why it could spread:** People will share funny/wrong “my phone thought I was trying to…” cards if correction is built in.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Proactive help can feel invasive unless it stays local and restrained.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong personal-agent canon and mobile UX fit.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**.

### 5. Reference Drift Ledger for AI Video

- **Trend signal:** AI video editing is moving toward multi-shot consistency, instruction editing, and real-time/open-ended changes, making drift easier to create and harder to review.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** `MSEditor` targets consistent multi-shot video editing; `Qwen-Video-Edit` and `JoyAI-Video-Edit` indicate active instruction-video-editing work.
- **Gap:** Creators need a small review artifact that records which identity/style/story invariants survived across shots and which drifted.
- **Smallest prototype:** Upload or describe a 10–20 second edit; produce a ledger with protected references, allowed changes, per-shot drift notes, and next edit instruction.
- **Why it could spread:** Before/after drift cards are easy to share with collaborators and clients.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Automated drift judgment may be too subjective; first version should be human-in-the-loop.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong fit for creative tools, reference contracts, Pantom/Orior media workflows, and taste systems.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**.

### 6. Video Try-On Fit Motion Receipt

- **Trend signal:** Video virtual try-on papers are reducing reliance on handcrafted spatial priors and moving toward end-to-end/instruction-driven garment transfer.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** `InstructVVT` and `UniVVT` both appeared in the August 2026 arXiv scan and target video virtual try-on without heavy inference-time spatial priors.
- **Gap:** Fashion try-on demos show a rendered look but rarely explain where motion, fit, sleeve behavior, or garment identity may be unreliable.
- **Smallest prototype:** A receipt card for a try-on clip: preserved body/motion, garment transfer claims, likely artifacts, unknown fit zones, and “do not buy from this alone” warning.
- **Why it could spread:** Shoppers and creators can share fit-motion receipts instead of just glam try-on outputs.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Without ground-truth physical fit, receipts may only describe visual plausibility.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong fashion/taste-engine lane with restrained trust UX.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**.

### 7. MCP Tool Path Inspector

- **Trend signal:** MCP/tool-server ecosystems are exploding and agents increasingly choose tools mid-task.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** GitHub API search for `mcp server created:>2025-01-01` returned high-star repositories including `microsoft/playwright-mcp` and `github/github-mcp-server`.
- **Gap:** Users see that an agent used a tool, but not the procedural path: why that tool, what authority it had, what state it read, and what residue it left.
- **Smallest prototype:** A local inspector card for one agent task showing selected tools, rejected tools, permissions, state reads, writes, and residue receipt.
- **Why it could spread:** Developers can attach tool-path cards to agent bug reports and PRs.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Tool telemetry formats may fragment across runtimes.
- **Fit for Max:** Direct fit for Hermes/Aven, capability receipts, package quarantine, and credential escrow.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**.

### 8. Local Agent Workspace Flight Bag

- **Trend signal:** Local-first agent workspaces are becoming a recognizable developer product surface.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** GitHub API scan for `local first app created:>2025-01-01` returned high-star agent/workspace repositories including `EverMind-AI/EverOS`, `holaboss-ai/holaOS`, and `deer-flow/llm-space`.
- **Gap:** Local workspaces still scatter task state across chats, files, tools, memories, and browser sessions.
- **Smallest prototype:** A portable “flight bag” folder for one task: task model, state barometer, context budget, credentials used, receipts, and next-action card.
- **Why it could spread:** People want to move agent work between machines/sessions without losing the operational state.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Could become a folder convention rather than a product unless it materially improves resumption.
- **Fit for Max:** Strong local-first/persistent-agent primitive.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**.

### 9. UAM Merge Slot Explainer

- **Trend signal:** Urban air mobility research is modeling corridor merge coordination with ETA gaps, trajectory bounds, and authority approval before corridor entry.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** `ETA Coordination at UAM Corridor Merging Points Using Worst-Case and Stochastic Trajectory Bounds` was published 2026-08-17.
- **Gap:** Civil autonomy interfaces need to explain why a vehicle got a merge slot without making the card look like operational clearance to the public.
- **Smallest prototype:** Simulator-only merge-slot explainer: requested ETA, required gap, uncertainty bound type, approval state, stale-data warning, and “not clearance” label.
- **Why it could spread:** Drone/AAM teams need non-specialist cards for demos, design reviews, and public trust conversations.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Regulatory and operational realities may make simplified cards misleading.
- **Fit for Max:** Good N1/Notrex-adjacent aviation interface lane; keep simulator-only.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**.

### 10. Synthetic Weather Dataset Diff Viewer

- **Trend signal:** Drone detection datasets are adding synthetic seasonal and weather variation to cover hard-to-sample operational conditions.
- **Existing proof:** **Verified:** `Beyond Clear Skies` introduces SynDroneVision-Weather with 55,187 annotated high-resolution images across seasonal/weather/domain shifts.
- **Gap:** Teams need to understand what synthetic variation adds and where it may create false confidence before using it for detection claims.
- **Smallest prototype:** A dataset diff viewer comparing clear/weather/season variants with labels for domain shift, annotation consistency, and deployment caveats.
- **Why it could spread:** Researchers and builders can share clear “what changed in the dataset” cards rather than only benchmark tables.
- **Risk / why it may be fake:** Synthetic realism may not transfer to actual sensors, cities, or adverse-weather physics.
- **Fit for Max:** Fits drone evidence cards, dataset critique, and conservative autonomy research.
- **Evidence label:** **Supported**.
